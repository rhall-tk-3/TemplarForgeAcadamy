Week 7

The Council of Troyes and Bernard of Clairvaux

The Council of Troyes gave the early Templars formal structure. Bernard of Clairvaux then became one of the order's most important defenders in writing.

What happened at Troyes?

The Council of Troyes opened on 13 January 1129 under the authority of the pope's legate. There the Templars received an approved rule of life and clearer institutional standing within Latin Christendom.

Some older works give the year as 1128 because of differences in medieval calendar dating. In modern usage, 13 January 1129 is standard.

Why the Rule mattered

The primitive Templar Rule set out how members were supposed to live: with discipline, humility, obedience, and readiness for military service. The order's identity as both religious and martial became much clearer after Troyes.

Bernard of Clairvaux

Bernard of Clairvaux was one of the most influential churchmen of the twelfth century. He supported the Templars and is closely linked with the order's early legitimation. He later wrote In Praise of the New Knighthood, presenting the Templars as a new kind of disciplined Christian warrior.

How to read Bernard's text responsibly

Bernard's treatise is not neutral reporting. It is advocacy written on behalf of the fledgling order. Students should treat it as evidence for how supporters wanted the Templars to be understood.

Two different kinds of evidence

Source type What it tells us
Council and rule How the church formally structured the early order.
Bernard's praise text How leading supporters promoted the Templars to a wider audience.

Short timeline

  1. 1127: Hugh de Payns seeks support in Europe.
  2. 13 January 1129: Council of Troyes opens.
  3. After Troyes: the Templar Rule gives formal shape to community life.
  4. Early 1130s: Bernard's praise of the "new knighthood" helps defend the order's mission.

Key terms

Council of Troyes
A church council that formalized the early Templar order and its rule.
Rule
A written set of regulations for religious community life.
Bernard of Clairvaux
Cistercian abbot and influential supporter of the early Templars.
