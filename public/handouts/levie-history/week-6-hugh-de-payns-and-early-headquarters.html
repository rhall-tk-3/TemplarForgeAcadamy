Week 6

Hugh de Payns and the Early Headquarters

Hugh de Payns was a leading founder of the Knights Templar. The early group was small and poor, and its first mission was the protection of Christian pilgrims traveling in the Kingdom of Jerusalem.

Who was Hugh de Payns?

Hugh de Payns (also written Hugues de Payens or Payns) was a French knight from Champagne. He became the first known master of the group that later became the Knights Templar.

Founding of the early community

According to standard reference accounts, Hugh de Payns and a small group of French knights vowed in late 1119 or early 1120 to protect pilgrims and to form a religious community for that purpose. Sources often describe the group as eight or nine knights led by Hugh.

Careful wording matters: some retellings give a fixed number and a fixed founding year, but responsible handouts should say late 1119 or early 1120 and note that medieval evidence is not perfectly uniform.

The early headquarters

King Baldwin II of Jerusalem gave the group quarters in a wing of the royal palace in the area associated by Latin Christians with the former Temple of Solomon. Because of that location, the order became known as the "Poor Knights of Christ and of the Temple of Solomon," or Templars.

Plain-language note: their name came from where they were housed in Jerusalem, not from a claim that they rebuilt the biblical Temple.

What early Templar life looked like

  • The group began as a small brotherhood, not a large army.
  • Its public purpose was to protect pilgrims on dangerous roads.
  • Its early reputation stressed poverty and discipline.
  • It depended on support from rulers, nobles, and churchmen.

Growth and recruitment

In 1127 Hugh traveled in Europe to seek backing for the order. This tour helped attract donations and noble support, which made the community larger and more stable.

Mini timeline

  1. 1099: Jerusalem comes under crusader rule.
  2. Late 1119 or early 1120: Hugh and companions organize to protect pilgrims.
  3. Early 1120s: the group lives in quarters near the Temple area in Jerusalem.
  4. 1127: Hugh tours Europe to gain support.

Key terms

Hugh de Payns
French knight and leading founder of the early Templar community.
Baldwin II
King of Jerusalem who granted quarters to the early Templars.
Temple of Solomon
The biblical association used by Latin Christians for the Jerusalem area that gave the Templars their name.
