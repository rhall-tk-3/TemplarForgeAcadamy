Syllabus Summary

Captain School is described as the transition from dependable junior officer to responsible steward of a local body: a man who can organize operations, preserve standards, mentor lieutenants, coordinate resources, care for the brothers under his charge, and sustain the life of the Order at the local level. The historical model named in the syllabus is the Commander / Preceptor, presented as the clearest model for local-command leadership.

Local Command Stewardship

Responsibility for operations, standards, people, and resources.

Leadership Under Authority

Govern a local body in alignment with mission and higher authority.

Leader Development

Mentor lieutenants and form future officers intentionally.

Promotion Readiness

Show readiness for wider oversight through tested local-command leadership.

Evaluation and Board Review

The syllabus evaluates attendance and participation, written assignments, operations planning, delegation and mentoring work, the local-command practicum, senior observation, final portfolio, and the oral board. It also requires one approved practicum such as planning and leading a monthly command cycle, organizing a service project, coordinating a local officer team for an event or ceremony, establishing a training rhythm for lieutenants, or leading a membership-development effort.

Attendance and Participation

10% — presence, preparedness, engagement, contribution to discussion.

Written Assignments

20% — historical understanding, reflection quality, practical application.

Local-Command Practicum

20% — execution, communication, adaptability, and follow-through.

Oral Board / Final Review

10% — officer presence, judgment, historical grounding, readiness for Captaincy.

Sample Oral Board Questions
  • Why were commanderies essential to the historical Templar Order?
  • What distinguishes a Captain from a Lieutenant in your Order?
  • Why is the Commander / Preceptor the right model for Captain School?
  • What must a Captain protect in a local body besides outward efficiency?
  • How do you know whether you are truly delegating rather than merely assigning tasks?
  • What does care of brothers look like at the local-command level?
  • How should a Captain respond when standards are slipping without becoming harsh or reactive?
  • How will you know whether your lieutenants are actually growing?
  • Where is your greatest weakness as a future Captain, and how are you addressing it?
  • Why are you ready—or not yet ready—for local command?
