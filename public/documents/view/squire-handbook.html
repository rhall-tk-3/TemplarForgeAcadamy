Squire Program Handbook
Templar Forge Academy — 8-Week Formation Program

Purpose Statement

This handbook establishes a formal schooling system for the Knights of The Kingdom of Christ Order. The school exists to form members in history, discipline, virtue, service, leadership, and a truthful understanding of the medieval Knights Templar. The school is educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose, cultivating a lifelong dedication to Christian knighthood.

Legitimacy and Scope

The Knights of The Kingdom of Christ Order is not military or paramilitary in nature, and all internal ranks are organizational and instructional only. While historical symbolism may be used to honor our heritage, false lineage claims or invented authority are strictly prohibited. The authority of this Order is derived entirely from its present commitment to Jesus Christ and its faithful adherence to Scripture.

Squire-Only Scope: This specific handbook applies exclusively to the Squire School, functioning as the foundational youth track of the Order's educational system.

Historical Foundation

The curriculum of the Squire School teaches truthful medieval Templar history alongside Christian moral formation. We honor the historical reality of the Order by focusing on their early mission of protecting pilgrims and living a life of dedicated service. The school focuses on historical facts and moral lessons without glorifying violence or spreading unverified historical legends.

Adult and Youth Separation & Safety

To ensure a safe and supportive learning environment, Squires have their own age-appropriate school track distinct from adult formations. This separation guarantees parental awareness, youth-safe oversight, and moderated discussions. All instruction, reading assignments, and service

expectations are carefully calibrated to be suitable to the age, comprehension, and maturity of the youth involved.

Core Principles

Squires are called to embody the highest ideals of Christian virtue. The core principles of the Order, clearly grounded in our founding documents, include:

  • Service Before Status: A true Squire seeks to serve rather than to be served.
  • Honesty and Truthfulness: A commitment to absolute truth in all words and actions.
  • Courage and Obedience: The bravery to do what is right, paired with joyful submission to legitimate authority.
  • Charity and Humility: Love for one's neighbor and the recognition that all gifts come from God.
  • Discipline and Accountability: Self-mastery over one's impulses and a willingness to be held to the Order's standards.
  • Visible Virtue: Leaders and aspiring leaders are held to a higher standard of visible virtue. No superior can command an act violating Scripture or civil law, and no Squire is permitted to obey such a command.

Squire School Eight-Week Program Structure

The Squire School is built upon an intensive eight-week track. Each week introduces specific historical and moral topics designed to foster both intellectual understanding and spiritual growth. Reading logs serve as proof of coursework completion and active participation, and must be strictly maintained.

Week 1: The First Crusade and the Call to Virtue

  • Weekly Focus: The historical context of the Holy Land and the fundamental virtues of the Order.
  • Topics of Instruction: The First Crusade in simple terms; why pilgrims traveled to Jerusalem; the immediate historical need for the formation of the Order; the foundational virtues of honesty, courage, obedience, and charity.
  • Key Terms or Figures: Christian Pilgrims, Jerusalem, The First Crusade.
  • Memory Targets: The early Order was formed to protect travelers; honesty and charity are the bedrock of knighthood.
  • Readiness Goals: Demonstrate a basic understanding of why the Order was necessary in medieval times.
  • Suggested Discussion/Activity: Discuss the dangers pilgrims faced and how courage and obedience help us protect the vulnerable today.

Week 2: The Founders and the Temple Mount

  • Weekly Focus: The actual historical founders and distinguishing historical fact from popular legend.
  • Topics of Instruction: Hugh de Payns and the first companions; the patronage of King Baldwin II; establishing the Temple Mount headquarters; identifying fact versus legend.
  • Key Terms or Figures: Hugh de Payns, King Baldwin II, Temple Mount.
  • Memory Targets: Hugh de Payns is the primary founder (circa 1119/1120); the Order did not begin with wealth or glory, but in humble service.
  • Readiness Goals: Accurately identify the founding figures and reject unverified myths regarding the Order's origins.
  • Suggested Discussion/Activity: Review simple biographies of the founders and discuss the importance of seeking historical truth.

Week 3: Official Recognition and the Religious Life

  • Weekly Focus: The formal recognition of the Order by the Church and the nature of religious vows.
  • Topics of Instruction: The Council of Troyes; the spiritual support of Bernard of Clairvaux; what constitutes a religious order; establishing a memory timeline from 1095 to 1139.
  • Key Terms or Figures: Council of Troyes, Bernard of Clairvaux, Religious Order Identity.
  • Memory Targets: The Council of Troyes (1129) gave official approval; a Templar was a monk before a warrior.
  • Readiness Goals: Differentiate between the informal founding and formal recognition, and recite the basic timeline.
  • Suggested Discussion/Activity: Map out the timeline on paper and discuss what it means to live a life dedicated to prayer.

Week 4: Structure, Symbols, and Discipline

  • Weekly Focus: How the Order was organized and the meaning behind its uniform and ranks.
  • Topics of Instruction: The classes of knight, sergeant, and chaplain; offices including Grand Master, Seneschal, Marshal, and Preceptor; organizational clothing, symbols, and discipline; the principle of service before self.
  • Key Terms or Figures: Grand Master, Preceptor, Sergeant, Red Cross.
  • Memory Targets: The red cross signifies sacrifice; leadership in the Order means serving those beneath you.
  • Readiness Goals: Identify the functional classes and historic offices, understanding that rank represents responsibility, not superiority.
  • Suggested Discussion/Activity: Analyze the historical clothing and symbols, discussing how modern Squires can visually represent Christian discipline.

Week 5: Logistics, Charity, and Daily Routine

  • Weekly Focus: The daily life of a Templar and the establishment of a personal moral routine.
  • Topics of Instruction: Castles, roads, and the logistics of pilgrim protection; prayer and routine daily life; charity and almsgiving; building a personal rule of conduct.
  • Key Terms or Figures: Almsgiving, Rule of Conduct, Pilgrim Roads.
  • Memory Targets: Daily prayer and discipline anchored the knight's soul; charity was required, not optional.
  • Readiness Goals: Draft a simple, personal rule of conduct detailing daily prayer and service expectations.
  • Suggested Discussion/Activity: Discuss the concept of a "Rule of Life" and how a scheduled routine prevents moral failure.

Week 6: Power, Danger, and Mission Drift

  • Weekly Focus: The ethical dangers of wealth, power, and losing sight of one's original purpose.
  • Topics of Instruction: Why the Templars became powerful; how reputation and wealth became dangerous; the fall of Acre in 1291; the concept of mission drift.
  • Key Terms or Figures: Fall of Acre (1291), Mission Drift, Stewardship.
  • Memory Targets: Wealth and power are severe moral tests; the loss of the Holy Land caused a crisis of purpose.
  • Readiness Goals: Explain how an organization can lose its way when it forgets its original charitable mission.
  • Suggested Discussion/Activity: Discuss modern examples of mission drift and how to stay true to one's foundational Christian calling.

Week 7: Crisis, False Accusation, and Courage

  • Weekly Focus: The suppression of the Order and the virtues required to face injustice.
  • Topics of Instruction: King Philip IV and the 1307 arrests; Pope Clement V and the suppression of the Order; the fate of Jacques de Molay; themes of justice, facing false

accusation, and maintaining courage.

  • Key Terms or Figures: Philip IV, Clement V, Jacques de Molay, 1307.
  • Memory Targets: The arrests occurred in 1307; courage means standing fast in the truth even when falsely accused.
  • Readiness Goals: Articulate the political and financial reasons behind the Order's suppression.
  • Suggested Discussion/Activity: Discuss how a Christian should respond to slander, false accusations, and institutional injustice.

Week 8: Review, Recitation, and Readiness

  • Weekly Focus: Final consolidation of historical knowledge and practical demonstration of readiness.
  • Topics of Instruction: Full review of the timeline; oral recitation and map work; service project presentations; preparation for the promotion board review.
  • Key Terms or Figures: Comprehensive Timeline, Service Project.
  • Memory Targets: Complete recall of the 8-week historical journey and the Order's core virtues.
  • Readiness Goals: Successfully present a service project and complete the oral recitation to the satisfaction of the Schoolmaster.
  • Suggested Discussion/Activity: Conduct mock oral boards and peer reviews of the service project presentations.

Student Standards and Required Records

Consistent participation and coursework completion are primary metrics for determining a Squire's readiness for advancement. Deficiencies in conduct or coursework may delay advancement, subject to Board review. To track these metrics, the school requires the following documentation:

  • Attendance Logs: Accurate records of participation in all required classes and meetings.
  • Reading Logs: Maintained by the Squire and verified weekly; serves as proof of coursework completion.
  • Service Logs: A record of charitable hours and practical service rendered to the community.
  • Assessment Sheets & Oral-Board Notes: Evaluative notes generated by Schoolmasters during reviews.
  • Advancement Recommendations: Official endorsements submitted for the Squire's promotion review.

Assessment and the Five Pillars

Every student shall be assessed consistently by their Schoolmaster on the following Five Pillars:

  • Knowledge: An accurate grasp of truthful history, Biblical principles, and organizational rules.
  • Judgment: The ability to apply discernment, common sense, and moral clarity to situations.
  • Service: Demonstrated willingness to put the needs of others before one's own comfort.
  • Character: Visible virtue, humility, discipline, and absolute honesty in all dealings.
  • Communication: The capacity to speak truth with honor, clarity, and respect during oral recitations and daily fellowship.

Promotion Readiness and Advancement: Squire to Sergeant

The transition from the youth Squire track to the adult ranks is a solemn milestone. Advancement from Squire to Sergeant at the age of 18 is not automatic. While completion of the curriculum is a strict requirement, the Board of Promotion Review evaluates overall readiness based upon coursework completion, logged attendance, active participation, and a demonstrated conduct becoming of a member.

Specific requirements for advancement to Sergeant include:

  • Attainment of the minimum age of eighteen.
  • Full completion of the eight-week Squire School curriculum.
  • Demonstrated strong attendance throughout the term.
  • A passing score on all written work and final review.
  • A successful oral interview before the board.
  • Meeting the mandatory service-hour threshold.
  • Completion and verification of the official Reading Log.
  • A formal signature agreeing to the adult Code of Conduct and Order Statutes.

Schoolmaster Standards for Squire Instruction

Those entrusted with teaching Squires bear a heavy spiritual responsibility. Schoolmasters must ensure youth-safe oversight at all times and facilitate strictly moderated discussions. Instructors must verify reading and attendance logs weekly. Teaching must be age-appropriate, grounded in historical truth, and devoid of violence glorification or unverified legends. Above all, a Schoolmaster must guide Squires with firm love, maintaining strict accountability while demonstrating unfailing Christian charity and humility.

Recommended Reading and Study Supports

To support the eight-week curriculum, Squires will utilize safe, order-approved materials. These generic source-based references include:

  • Selections from the official Rules and Statutes of the Order.
  • Simple, historically accurate biographies of foundational figures (e.g., Hugh de Payns, King Baldwin II).
  • Provided History Handouts covering the Crusades, logistics, and the suppression.
  • Maps of the Holy Land, pilgrim routes, and timeline graphics.
  • Memory work worksheets for key dates and core virtues.
  • Optional spiritual formation readings focused on basic Christian disciplines, prayer, and character building.

Suggested Annual Cycle for Squire School

To maintain momentum and continuous engagement, the Squire School operates on a suggested annual calendar:

  • Two Major Eight-Week Terms: The primary instructional blocks (e.g., Spring and Autumn).
  • One Summer Review Program: A lighter, fellowship-based review of history and outdoor activities.
  • One Chapter Lecture Series: A special event featuring guest instruction on history or theology.
  • One Annual Service Campaign: A coordinated, Order-wide charitable project driven by the Squires.
  • One School Banquet or Awards Ceremony: A formal gathering to recognize advancements, service milestones, and character achievements.

Final Note:

The Squire School of the Knights of The Kingdom of Christ Order is dedicated to forging the next generation of faithful servants. Through the study of truthful history, the expectation of rigorous Christian conduct, and a selfless dedication to charity, we equip our youth to bear the cross of Christ with honor, courage, and unwavering truth.
