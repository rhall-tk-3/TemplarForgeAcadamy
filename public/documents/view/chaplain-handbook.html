Chaplain Corps School Program
Official Program Handbook — 6-Week Certification Course

Program Purpose

The KTKC Chaplain Corps School Program prepares qualified members of the Order for chaplaincy service through formal instruction, guided prayer practice, pastoral care training, ethical formation, and final assessment. This is a spiritually serious program. The office of chaplain is not ceremonial — it is the living continuation of the oldest spiritual tradition in the history of the Knights Templar.

From the moment the Rule of the Temple was approved at the Council of Troyes in 1129, chaplains were recognized as one of the three essential classes of the Order. Chaplains celebrated Mass, heard confession, administered last rites, sat with the dying, and kept the community anchored to its sacred purpose. Without chaplains, the Templar Order was a military force. With chaplains, it was a religious order. That distinction is as vital today as it was in the Holy Land.

"Lead with prayer. Serve with compassion. Speak the truth. Keep confidence. Stand firm against spiritual abuse. Reflect the character of Christ in all that you do for the Order and for those in your care." — Model Charge to a KTKC Chaplain, Article XIV, KTKC Chaplain Corps Manual

Program Overview

  • Duration: 6 weeks
  • Program Type: Specialist Corps Certification
  • Audience: Adult KTKC members called to chaplaincy service
  • Certification Term: 1 year, subject to annual review and renewal
  • Passing Score: 70% on all weekly assessments and final exam

Program-Level Learning Objectives

  1. Explain the identity, authority, mission, and limits of the KTKC Chaplain Corps.
  2. Describe the historic role of chaplains in the original Knights Templar from 1129 to the Order's suppression.
  3. Apply the historic-to-modern correlation standard to present-day chaplaincy practice.
  4. Identify the duties of the Master Chaplain and Assistant Chaplain.
  5. Lead public prayer, invocation, and benediction with reverence and clarity.
  6. Read and apply Holy Scripture appropriately in ceremonial and pastoral settings.
  7. Provide basic pastoral care including ministry of presence, visitation, and grief support.
  8. Protect confidentiality while recognizing when reporting is required.
  9. Identify and avoid spiritual abuse; report it when encountered.
  10. Complete required documentation following KTKC standard operating procedures.
  11. Serve with dignity and solemnity in all ceremonial settings.

Practicum Requirements

In addition to weekly written assignments and assessments, each chaplain candidate must complete the following practicum requirements before certification:

  • Minimum 3 opening or closing prayers (led in a conclave, meeting, or formal setting)
  • Minimum 2 Scripture readings (public, formal setting)
  • Minimum 2 pastoral contacts or visitations (documented per SOP 3)
  • Minimum 1 devotional or exhortation delivery (2–5 minutes, formal setting)
  • Minimum 1 memorial, bereavement, or hospital-support exercise
  • Minimum 1 ethics or reporting case exercise (written or oral)

Weekly Curriculum

Week One
The Calling: Identity, Authority, and the Templar Chaplain
The history and identity of the Templar chaplain from 1129 to today; the KTKC Chaplain Corps mission; offices of Master and Assistant Chaplain; certification vs. ordination.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain the identity, mission, and authority of the KTKC Chaplain Corps.
  • Distinguish between certification and ordination, and between chaplaincy and command authority.
  • Describe the role of chaplains in the original Knights Templar — their place, duties, and importance.
  • Identify the offices of Master Chaplain and Assistant Chaplain and their responsibilities.
  • State why the chaplaincy is a spiritually serious office, not a ceremonial ornament.

Key Terms

  • KTKC Chaplain Corps — The formally established body within the KTKC charged with spiritual leadership, prayer, pastoral care, and biblical encouragement for all members.
  • Master Chaplain — The primary chaplaincy officer; responsible for leading devotions, guiding prayer, pastoral counsel, and the overall spiritual life of the community.
  • Certification vs. Ordination — Certification qualifies a member for chaplaincy service within the KTKC Order; ordination is an ecclesiastical act of a church — these are distinct and must not be confused.
  • Templar Chaplain (Historic) — One of the three recognized classes in the original Knights Templar; ordained priests who celebrated Mass, heard confession, administered last rites, and kept the Order spiritually anchored.
  • The Rule of the Temple (1129) — The founding governing document approved at the Council of Troyes that formally established the three classes of membership including chaplains.

Assignment

Write a 1–2 page Call-to-Service Reflection: Why do you believe you are called to chaplaincy within the KTKC? How does the historic Templar chaplain's example shape your understanding of this calling?

Week Two
The Foundation: Christian Identity, Chivalric Conduct, and Templar Spiritual Standards
The explicitly Christian foundation of KTKC chaplaincy; the five core standards of the office; the historic-to-modern correlation standard; daily Templar spiritual disciplines.

Learning Objectives

  • Articulate the Christian foundation of KTKC chaplaincy and why it is explicitly Christian, not generically spiritual.
  • Describe the five core standards: Reverence, Charity, Confidentiality, Discipline of Speech, and Christian Example.
  • Explain the historic-to-modern correlation standard and how the Templar daily rhythm of prayer applies to modern chaplaincy.
  • Write a personal conduct summary reflecting chivalric Christian standards.

The Five Core Standards

  • Reverence — Deep respect for God, sacred things, and persons at all times and in all settings.
  • Charity — Active, consistent care for the needs of others — especially the sick, struggling, bereaved, or in spiritual distress.
  • Confidentiality — Protecting what is shared in pastoral trust, with defined limits when safety requires reporting.
  • Discipline of Speech — Speaking truthfully, carefully, and constructively; rooted in the Templar Rule's emphasis on ordered speech and silence.
  • Christian Example — Personal conduct — in public and in private — visibly reflecting the Christian faith the chaplain represents.

Assignment

Write a one-page Christian Conduct Summary: What does a KTKC chaplain look like in daily life? Include at least three specific behavioral commitments grounded in the historic-to-modern correlation standard.

Week Three
The Voice: Public Prayer, Scripture Ministry, and Devotional Leadership
Leading formal prayer and benedictions; Scripture reading in ceremonial settings; preparing and delivering a brief devotional; standards of sacred decorum.

Learning Objectives

  • Lead a formal opening prayer with proper structure, tone, and brevity.
  • Lead a closing benediction appropriate to a KTKC conclave setting.
  • Read Holy Scripture aloud with clarity and reverence.
  • Prepare and deliver a brief devotional or exhortation drawn from Scripture.

The Five Elements of Formal Prayer

  1. Address — Speak to God with a proper title; approach with respect, not casualness.
  2. Acknowledgment — Briefly acknowledge who God is and why you come before Him.
  3. Petition — State what is being asked — guidance, blessing, protection for the men gathered.
  4. Alignment — Place the work under God's purposes; commit it to His direction and oversight.
  5. Close — A proper closing: in the name of Christ, or a traditional doxological close.

Assignment

Write three original prayers: (1) a formal opening invocation for a KTKC conclave, (2) a closing benediction, (3) a short prayer of dedication for a new officer installation. Each should demonstrate the five elements above.

Week Four
The Shepherd: Pastoral Care, Visitation, and Ministry of Presence
Ministry of presence as the foundation of pastoral care; historic Templar care for the sick and dying; visitation protocol (SOPs 2–4); escalation requirements.

Learning Objectives

  • Define "ministry of presence" and explain why the chaplain's first duty is often to listen, not to speak.
  • Describe the historic Templar care for the sick and dying and its connection to modern visitation ministry.
  • Follow proper protocol for home visits, hospital visits, and bereavement support.
  • Identify situations requiring immediate escalation beyond pastoral care.

KTKC Pastoral Care SOPs

  • SOP 2 — Distress Intake: Listen carefully; confirm permission to follow up; log concern appropriately; do not rush to fix.
  • SOP 3 — Home or Hospital Visit: Conduct with a second member present when possible; offer prayer if welcomed; document briefly.
  • SOP 4 — Funeral Support: Coordinate with family and officiating clergy; ensure Order's presence is respectful; follow up with bereavement care.

Escalation Thresholds (Non-Negotiable)

  • Any stated or implied threat of self-harm
  • Any disclosure of intent to harm another person
  • Any disclosure of illegal activity affecting others
  • Any accusation of spiritual abuse or misconduct by a leader

Assignment

Prepare a Pastoral Visitation Plan for a KTKC member who has been hospitalized and has not attended the conclave in six weeks. Outline what you will do, in what order, and why.

Week Five
The Conscience: Ethics, Confidentiality, Boundaries, and Reporting
Chaplain confidentiality and its limits; defining and identifying spiritual abuse; pastoral boundaries; writing factual incident reports; non-interference after escalation.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain the chaplain's duty of confidentiality and when it must give way to reporting.
  • Define spiritual abuse and explain how to identify and prevent it.
  • State what constitutes proper boundaries in chaplaincy relationships.
  • Write a factual, non-speculative incident report following KTKC standards.

Spiritual Abuse Defined

Spiritual abuse is the misuse of spiritual authority or sacred language to manipulate, silence, intimidate, or control another person. Examples include: claiming "God told me" to override a member's objection; using prayer or Scripture as intimidation; imposing spiritual guilt to prevent reporting misconduct.

Incident Report Standards

  • Brief — No unnecessary detail or narrative beyond what is required
  • Factual — No speculation about motivations; report only what was directly stated or observed
  • Dated — Accurate date and time
  • Clear — Understandable to anyone reading it without prior context
  • Filed with the Commander per SOP 2

Assignment

Write an Ethics Response Paper: Scenario — during a pastoral conversation, a member discloses that an officer has been using spiritual language to silence his legitimate concerns. Describe step by step what you will do, what you will write in the incident report, and how you will protect the member's safety without interfering in the formal review.

Week Six
The Commission: Ceremony, Observances, and Chaplain Readiness
Chaplain's role in KTKC ceremony; planning an order of service; capstone integration across all five core standards; the living legacy of the Templar chaplaincy; commissioning readiness.

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the chaplain's role in KTKC ceremonial settings and formal observances.
  • Plan and draft an order of service for a KTKC Christian observance.
  • Demonstrate integrated readiness across all five core chaplain standards.
  • Articulate the historic significance of the Templar chaplaincy and its living legacy in the KTKC.
  • Complete the capstone self-assessment and prepare for commissioning.

Planning an Order of Service

A chaplain planning a Christian observance must consider:

  1. The Occasion — Its specific spiritual significance (installation, memorial, seasonal observance)
  2. Scripture Selection — Appropriate texts matched to the occasion's tone and purpose
  3. Tone — Joyful, solemn, commissioning, or commemorative
  4. Prayers Required — Invocation, responsive elements if applicable, benediction
  5. Flow Integration — The chaplain's parts integrate with the Order's formal proceedings; the chaplain serves the ceremony, not the other way around

The Living Legacy

The KTKC chaplain carries an unbroken line of purpose from the 1129 Rule of the Temple. The historic chaplain's greatest legacy: in an Order that could easily have become purely military, the chaplain was the reason it remained a religious order. You are that chaplain today. You are the keeper of the Order's spiritual life — the voice that calls the brethren to prayer, the presence at the bedside of the sick, the conscience that will not allow spiritual abuse to go unchallenged.

Capstone Assignment

Write a full Capstone Reflection: How has studying the history of the Templar chaplain shaped how you understand your own calling? What will you do differently as a KTKC chaplain because of what you have learned in these six weeks? Include specific references to the historic Templar tradition, the five core standards, and the Model Charge (Article XIV).

Certification Standards

Certification requires successful completion of all of the following:

  • All six weekly written assignments submitted and assessed
  • All practicum requirements completed and documented
  • Weekly quiz average of 70% or higher
  • Written certification examination passed at 70% or higher
  • Oral board review completed with satisfactory result
  • Schoolmaster certification signature
