Readiness Goal

Trace the steps from a small band of knights to a formally recognized religious order.

Historical Snapshot

Britannica records that in late 1119 or early 1120, Hugh de Payns and eight or nine French knights vowed themselves to the protection of pilgrims and formed a religious community for that purpose. King Baldwin II of Jerusalem gave them quarters in a wing of the royal palace in the area of the former Temple of Solomon, which gave the Order its name. Beginning in 1127, Hugh sought support across Europe, where nobles donated to the cause. The Council of Troyes in 1128 gave further sanction to the Order, Bernard of Clairvaux defended and advanced its cause, and in 1139 Pope Innocent II granted major privileges that placed the Templars under the pope alone.

Trace the Steps

  1. Hugh de Payns and his companions vowed themselves to pilgrim protection.
  2. Baldwin II gave them recognized quarters in Jerusalem.
  3. Hugh gained noble support in Europe beginning in 1127.
  4. The Council of Troyes sanctioned the Order in 1128.
  5. Bernard of Clairvaux defended the new knighthood and strengthened its legitimacy.
  6. Pope Innocent II granted papal privileges in 1139.

Formation Insight

The Order did not mature through private ambition. It matured through recognized authority, right order, and spiritual legitimacy.
