Templar Forge Academy — 8-Week Formation Program

Purpose of the Academy and Corporal Program

This handbook establishes the curriculum and operational guidelines for the Corporal program of the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ - Templar Forge Academy. The Academy is educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose. The Corporal program serves as the next formal stage of adult formation, building upon the foundational knowledge acquired during the introductory phase of membership.

It is paramount to understand that the Order and its Academy are not military or paramilitary in nature. All internal ranks, including that of Corporal, are organizational and instructional only. The program exists to forge individuals of strong moral character dedicated to charitable works and a truthful appreciation of historical facts, completely devoid of martial or paramilitary posturing.

Mission and Historical Foundation

The primary mission of the Templar Forge Academy is to cultivate a truthful understanding of the medieval Knights Templar, stripping away centuries of myth to reveal the core reality of their service. The Order is forever consecrated to Jesus Christ, and this dedication shapes our modern mission to provide a truthful, nonviolent witness to His gospel through self-sacrifice and fellowship.

The historical foundation of the Corporal program delves deeper into the reality of the original Templar mission. We examine their roots in the First Crusade, their rapid growth, the complexity of their daily discipline, the inherent risks of their acquired wealth, and the ultimate tragedy of their suppression, extracting lasting lessons for modern Christian leadership.

Core Principles

The Corporal program demands adherence to a strict set of core principles that govern the conduct, study, and daily lives of our members. Candidates are trained to embody:

  • Truth before legend: Committing to rigorous, factual historical study over popular, romanticized fabrications.
  • Service before status: Embracing the principle of service before self, viewing advancement as a call to heavier burdens, not greater privileges.
  • Discipline before display: Emphasizing internal spiritual mastery, prayer, and obedience over outward organizational pageantry.
  • Charity before privilege: Ensuring that the resources and efforts of the Order are directed toward those in need.
  • Leadership through accountability: Recognizing that no member is above reproach, and all are accountable to God, Scripture, and the fellowship of the Order.

Program Audience and Scope

The Corporal program is an 8-week adult formation program specifically designed for members who have successfully completed the Levie program. It transitions the candidate from a basic probationary understanding into a more rigorous study of the Order's history, spiritual disciplines, and the responsibilities inherent in holding organizational rank.

Program Standards and Legitimacy Measures

Institutional integrity is guarded through strict adherence to established program standards. The Academy utilizes signed enrollment records, direct Schoolmaster oversight, written course standards, structured rank boards, and an annual curriculum review to maintain the quality of formation. Reading requirements and mandatory service work are integrated directly into the curriculum.

The Templar Forge Academy enforces a clear and absolute rejection of false lineage claims, invented authority, theatrical succession claims, and any unofficial military style. We are an educational, historical, and charitable fraternity; any attempt to militarize the Order or claim direct, unbroken succession from the medieval Knights Templar is strictly prohibited.

Student Expectations and Records Maintained

Candidates in the Corporal program are expected to maintain the highest standards of participation, study, and service. To ensure these standards are met, the Academy requires the meticulous maintenance of the following records:

  • Attendance logs
  • Reading logs
  • Service logs
  • Assessment sheets
  • Oral-board notes
  • Advancement recommendations

Assessment Model

Throughout the 8-week program, candidates are evaluated based on five foundational pillars of assessment:

  • Knowledge: Mastery of the historical curriculum and organizational procedures.
  • Judgment: The exercise of mature Christian discernment in historical study and daily life.
  • Service: Active, documented participation in the charitable works of the Order.
  • Character: Consistent demonstration of discipline, humility, and moral integrity.
  • Communication: The ability to articulate the history and principles of the Order effectively and truthfully.

Full 8-Week Corporal Curriculum

Week 1: Why the Order Was Founded / Mission and Historical Need

Primary Focus

The First Crusade context, the danger to pilgrims, and the original protective mission.

Key Historical Subjects

Conditions in the Holy Land post-1099, pilgrim routes, and the immediate necessity for organized defense.

Formation Emphasis

Understanding the call to protect the vulnerable as the genesis of the Order.

Readiness Goal

Accurately describe the exact historical need that birthed the Templar mission.

Week 2: Founders, Early Support, and Legitimacy

Primary Focus

Hugh de Payns, the first companions, Baldwin II, the Temple Mount, Council of Troyes, and Bernard of Clairvaux.

Key Historical Subjects

The formalization of the Order, royal patronage, and the securing of papal legitimacy.

Formation Emphasis

The importance of legitimate authority, spiritual covering, and working within established frameworks.

Readiness Goal

Trace the steps from a small band of knights to a formally recognized religious order.

Week 3: Growth, Expansion, and Public Trust

Primary Focus

Growth of the Order, expansion across Christendom, castles, roads, stewardship, and public confidence.

Key Historical Subjects

The establishment of preceptories, the logistics of the Crusader states, and early financial stewardship.

Formation Emphasis

Building and maintaining public trust through unwavering integrity and competence.

Readiness Goal

Explain the logistical and administrative expansion of the medieval Order.

Week 4: Structure, Offices, and Daily Life

Primary Focus

Knight, sergeant, and chaplain classes; key offices such as Grand Master, Seneschal, Marshal, and Preceptor; daily discipline and routine life.

Key Historical Subjects

The Latin Rule, hierarchy, monastic schedules, and the separation of classes.

Formation Emphasis

Embracing structured discipline and understanding one's organizational role without ego.

Readiness Goal

Identify the major offices of the Order and describe the daily monastic routine of a Templar.

Week 5: Spiritual Discipline, Service, and the Corporal Rank

Primary Focus

Prayer, obedience, charity, disciplined conduct, service before self, and the responsibilities connected to the Corporal rank.

Key Historical Subjects

The inward spiritual life of the Order and its translation into outward service.

Formation Emphasis

Cultivating personal spiritual discipline as the absolute prerequisite for holding the Corporal rank.

Readiness Goal

Articulate the modern duties of a Corporal and demonstrate a commitment to service before self.

Week 6: Wealth, Power, Risks, and Mission Drift

Primary Focus

How the Order became powerful, the dangers of wealth and reputation, and the warning against drifting from mission.

Key Historical Subjects

International banking, vast land holdings, political entanglement, and the loss of the original focus.

Formation Emphasis

Vigilance against institutional and personal pride, and the spiritual dangers of unchecked power.

Readiness Goal

Critically assess how success and wealth became the vulnerabilities that compromised the Order.

Week 7: Crisis, Friday the 13th, and Suppression

Primary Focus

Philip IV, the 1307 arrests, Clement V, false accusation, suppression, and courage under pressure.

Key Historical Subjects

The political motivations of the French Crown, the trials, forced confessions, and papal dissolution.

Formation Emphasis

Maintaining faith, courage, and integrity in the face of false accusation and systemic injustice.

Readiness Goal

Summarize the events leading to the suppression of the Order without resorting to conspiracy theories.

Week 8: Jacques de Molay, Fall of the Order, and Historical Importance

Primary Focus

Final review, Jacques de Molay, the fall of the Order, historical importance, and readiness for promotion review.

Key Historical Subjects

The martyrdom of the last Grand Master, the dissolution of assets, and the true legacy of the Templars.

Formation Emphasis

Synthesizing the 8-week curriculum into a coherent understanding of historical truth and spiritual readiness.

Readiness Goal

Demonstrate full preparedness for the Corporal promotion review board.

  • Schoolmaster Guidance

Schoolmasters bear the burden of ensuring that promotion within the Templar Forge Academy is based strictly on faithfulness, discipline, active participation, and the successful completion of all requirements. Instructors must emphasize factual history over lore, charitable works over internal politics, and disciplined character over mere academic knowledge.

Schoolmasters are instructed to look for visible humility in all candidates. Deficiencies in conduct, a lack of charitable engagement, or incomplete coursework may delay promotion, subject to Board review. The goal is to form servants of Christ, not merely scholars of history.

Promotion Readiness Statement

Completion of the 8-week Corporal curriculum is a mandatory requirement for advancement; however, completion does not guarantee automatic promotion. The Board of Promotion Review evaluates each candidate holistically, heavily considering coursework completion, logged attendance, active participation, documented service, and conduct becoming of a member of the Order.

It is a foundational tenet of the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ that ranks cannot be bought or sold. Advancement is earned entirely through merit, demonstrated character, and a proven willingness to serve others.

Suggested Annual Academy Cycle

To provide structure and consistency for adult formation, the Corporal program operates on a suggested annual cycle:

  • Two major eight-week terms for advancing Levie graduates.
  • One review term dedicated to remediation, deep historical study, and fellowship.
  • One chapter lecture series bringing in advanced topics and guest instruction.
  • One annual service campaign where Corporals are expected to take active operational roles.
  • One recognition ceremony to formally elevate those who have met the standards of the Board.

Closing Affirmation

The Corporal Program of the Templar Forge Academy stands as a testament to our commitment to a truthful understanding of the medieval Knights Templar. We remain an organization that is educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose, forever consecrated to Jesus Christ. We affirm that service before self is our highest calling, and we declare unconditionally that no superior can command an act violating Scripture or civil law. Those who achieve the rank of Corporal, and those who lead them, are held to a higher standard of visible virtue and humility, ensuring that our Order remains a beacon of nonviolent, truthful witness in a complex world.
