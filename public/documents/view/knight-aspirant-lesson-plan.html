Knight Aspirant School — Lesson Plan Program
An Adult Formation Program — The Next Step After Sergeant First Class

Program Overview

The Knight Aspirant School is an intensive 8-week period of formation preparing adult candidates for the responsibilities of Christian knighthood. This program deliberately moves away from historical timelines and structural memorization, focusing entirely on inward spiritual discipline and the articulation of personal calling.

This 8-week program is divided into two distinct phases:
Weeks 1–4: Class instruction and guided spiritual formation.
Weeks 5–8: Writing, refining, and submitting the required Knight Aspirant essay.

The core philosophy of this program is that an aspirant is being prepared for Christian knighthood through inward discipline before they can ever be trusted with outward responsibility.

Knight Aspirant Standards and Spiritual Emphasis

The central, unmistakable emphasis of the Knight Aspirant program is this: A Templar is first a monk before a warrior.

Outward symbols, rank, and imagery mean absolutely nothing without inward conversion, Christian conduct, and mature obedience. Before a candidate may bear the title of Knight, they must demonstrate mastery over themselves. This formation relies heavily on:

  • Prayer and Scripture: The daily anchor of the aspirant’s life and the primary weapon of spiritual warfare.
  • Self-Mastery and Discipline: Governing one’s speech, impulses, and ego.
  • Charity and Humility: Serving others selflessly and rejecting vanity or the desire for prestige.
  • Obedience: Submission to godly authority and the Rule of the Order.
  • Spiritual Warfare before Outward Combat Imagery: Recognizing that the true battle is against sin, darkness, and spiritual forces, rather than earthly adversaries.
  • Peacemaking and Wise Restraint: Knowing exactly when to stand firm in truth, and when to walk away from foolish disputes.

Required Essay Specifications

During the final four weeks of this program, the aspirant must write an essay under the exact title:

“What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?”

This essay is not busywork; it is a profound statement of calling, a demonstration of discipline, and a proof of readiness. The essay must adhere to the following strict specifications:

FormatStandard college essay format
Cover PageFormal cover page bearing the paper title — required
SpacingSingle-spaced
LengthOne full page front and back if handwritten or printed double-sided, or two full typed pages if prepared in a Word document
SubmissionComplete, neat, readable, and turned in by the assigned deadline
ContentMust deeply reflect personal conviction, Christian maturity, an understanding of Templar virtues, and readiness for Knightly responsibility

Weekly Curriculum

Week 1 — Class Phase
The Knight Aspirant Calling — Monk Before Warrior

This week introduces the foundational concept of the aspirant journey. Before one can wear the mantle of a warrior, one must adopt the heart of a monk. Christian knighthood begins entirely with surrender to Christ.

Lesson Focus

Inward formation, the necessity of daily prayer, immersion in Scripture, and the embrace of humility.

Formation Emphasis

Recognizing that outward titles are hollow without an inward devotion to Christ and obedience to His Word.

Reflection Questions

  • In what areas of your life do you need to cultivate the “monk” (inward discipline) before you can act as the “warrior” (outward action)?
  • How does the concept of total surrender to Christ change your understanding of knighthood?

Discussion Questions

  • Why is humility considered the foundational virtue of a Templar Knight?
  • Discuss the dangers of seeking the title of Knight without possessing the inward devotion of a monk.

Readiness Goal

Establish a dedicated, unbroken daily rhythm of prayer and Scripture reading.

Week 2 — Class Phase
Spiritual Warfare, Self-Mastery, and Christian Conduct

A knight who cannot control his own temper, tongue, and impulses is a danger to the Order and a poor witness to Christ. This week focuses on the Armor of God and the absolute necessity of self-mastery.

Lesson Focus

Putting on the full armor of God; mastery over speech, anger, impulse, and vanity; distinguishing between righteous zeal and reckless behavior.

Formation Emphasis

The hardest battles are fought within. True spiritual warfare means conquering one’s own ego and worldly desires.

Reflection Questions

  • When are you most tempted to respond with reckless emotion rather than disciplined restraint?
  • Which piece of the Armor of God (Ephesians 6) do you feel you lack the most right now, and why?

Discussion Questions

  • What is the difference between righteous Christian zeal and reckless, ego-driven behavior?
  • How does a knight’s mastery over their speech impact the reputation of the entire Order?

Readiness Goal

Demonstrate visible restraint in speech and emotional response during conflicts or disagreements.

Week 3 — Class Phase
Charity, Brotherhood, Service, and Witness in Modern Society

Knighthood is not an isolated, historical fantasy; it is an active, modern calling to serve others. This week explores how ancient virtues are practically applied in today’s world through charity and brotherhood.

Lesson Focus

Practicing mercy, selfless service, active fellowship, protection of the weak, and maintaining a Christian witness in secular society.

Formation Emphasis

Templar virtues must transfer into ordinary, daily life — at home, at work, and in the community.

Reflection Questions

  • How can you practically protect the weak or serve the marginalized in your current modern context?
  • Is your daily conduct at work and home a true reflection of the Christian witness you profess?

Discussion Questions

  • Why is active participation in brotherhood/fellowship necessary for individual spiritual survival?
  • Discuss practical ways Templar virtues can be applied to ordinary, modern societal challenges without being theatrical.

Readiness Goal

Identify and execute a specific act of anonymous charity or selfless service this week.

Week 4 — Class Phase
Knightly Responsibility, Discernment, and Readiness

The final week of class instruction focuses on the weight of leadership and the discernment required of a Knight. It prepares the aspirant to transition from learning to articulating their calling.

Lesson Focus

Exercising mature judgment, knowing when to stand firm vs. when to withdraw, honoring authority, and rejecting ego-driven conflict.

Formation Emphasis

True strength is often found in restraint. A Knight must discern the difference between a battle worth fighting and a distraction meant to derail them.

Reflection Questions

  • Can you recall a time when withdrawing or remaining silent took more courage than fighting or arguing?
  • Are you spiritually and emotionally ready to bear the weight of Knightly responsibility?

Discussion Questions

  • How does a Knight determine when it is appropriate to stand firm versus when it is wise to walk away?
  • What does it mean to honor authority even when it requires sacrificing personal preference?

Readiness Goal

Prepare mentally and spiritually to begin drafting the Knight Aspirant essay.

Week 5 — Essay Phase
Essay Planning and Outline

Transitioning from classroom instruction to personal articulation. Aspirants will spend this week outlining their required essay, “What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?”

Weekly Task

Review the essay specifications. Draft a comprehensive outline covering historical understanding, spiritual calling, the “monk before warrior” ethos, and personal readiness.

Formation Emphasis

Clarity of thought reflects clarity of calling. Organizing the essay is an exercise in organizing one’s spiritual priorities.

Reflection Question

What is the single most important conviction you want to convey in your essay?

Discussion Question

How does outlining your thoughts help clarify your actual readiness for Knighthood?

Readiness Goal

Complete a structured, detailed outline for the essay.

Week 6 — Essay Phase
First Draft and Personal Conviction

Writing the first full draft. This week requires deep introspection, translating the lessons of the past weeks into a cohesive statement of personal conviction.

Weekly Task

Write the rough draft of the essay. Ensure it meets the length requirements (one page front/back handwritten, or two full pages typed).

Formation Emphasis

Vulnerability and honesty before God. Do not write what sounds impressive; write what is true to your calling.

Reflection Question

Does your draft reflect a desire to serve Christ, or a desire for a title?

Discussion Question

What part of the essay is proving the most difficult to write, and why?

Readiness Goal

Complete the first full draft of the essay.

Week 7 — Essay Phase
Revision, Clarity, and Alignment with Templar Virtues

A Knight must be precise. This week is dedicated to refining, editing, and polishing the essay to ensure it aligns perfectly with Templar virtues.

Weekly Task

Edit the essay for grammar, flow, and formatting. Ensure the cover page is correct and the document is single-spaced. Check that it clearly articulates the “monk before warrior” concept.

Formation Emphasis

Discipline in the small things. Presenting a neat, well-edited document demonstrates respect for the Order and the process.

Reflection Question

How does the discipline of revising and correcting your work mirror the discipline of spiritual repentance?

Discussion Question

Why is attention to detail (like formatting and grammar) an important trait for a Knight?

Readiness Goal

Finalize the essay draft, completely free of errors.

Week 8 — Essay Phase
Final Submission, Oral Readiness, and Review

The culmination of the Aspirant School. The essay is submitted, and the candidate prepares for the Board’s review of their overall conduct, attendance, and fellowship.

Weekly Task

Submit the final essay by the assigned deadline. Prepare to orally articulate the core points of your essay to leadership if asked.

Formation Emphasis

Standing ready. The aspirant has done the work and now submits to the judgment and timing of the Order’s leadership.

Reflection Question

Are you at peace with yielding your advancement entirely to the discernment of the Board of Promotion?

Discussion Question

How will you maintain your spiritual momentum regardless of when your knighting ceremony takes place?

Readiness Goal

Submit the final essay and maintain impeccable conduct during the review period.

Optional Supplemental Reading — Broader Knowledge Only

Not tested. Not required for promotion. For broader historical awareness and organizational understanding only.

To fully understand the broader tapestry of Templar history and modern Templar-inspired charitable organizations, candidates may optionally explore the historical context of women within the Order:

  • While the medieval Knights Templar was a monastic military order of men, it received significant spiritual and financial patronage from noblewomen who supported the Order’s survival and logistics.
  • There are historical records of women acting as associate sisters (sorores) or participating in the domestic and spiritual life of certain regional commanderies, dedicating themselves to prayer and the support of the Order.
  • Many modern, secular, and charitable Templar-inspired organizations (such as OSMTH) fully integrate women as Dames, recognizing that the modern battle is one of charity, diplomacy, and Christian witness, which requires the faithful service of both men and women.

Note to the Schoolmaster and Board of Promotion

Completion of this 8-week class instruction and the submission of the required essay supports a candidate’s promotion review. However, advancement to the rank of Knight is never automatic. It depends entirely on the candidate’s demonstrated Christian conduct, consistent attendance, active fellowship, visible discipline, and overall spiritual readiness as determined by leadership.
