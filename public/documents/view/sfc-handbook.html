Sergeant First Class Handbook

Purpose of the Academy and Sergeant First Class Handbook

This handbook establishes the operational guidelines, conduct expectations, and rigorous formation standards for the Sergeant First Class rank within the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ - Templar Forge Academy. The Academy is educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose. The Sergeant First Class program represents an advanced formation track designed to bridge the gap between general adult participation and the solemn vows of knighthood, establishing members as dependable, spiritually mature leaders within the Order.

It must be explicitly stated that the Order and its Academy are not military or paramilitary in nature; all internal ranks, including that of Sergeant First Class, are organizational and instructional only. The program exists solely to forge individuals of uncompromising moral character dedicated to charitable works, orderly administration, and rigorous spiritual formation, completely devoid of martial posturing or unofficial military style.

Mission and Historical Foundation

The primary mission of the Templar Forge Academy is to cultivate a truthful understanding of the medieval Knights Templar, rejecting conjecture and myth in favor of genuine historical appreciation and spiritual emulation. The Order is forever consecrated to Jesus Christ, and this dedication shapes our modern mission to provide a truthful, nonviolent witness to His gospel through sacrificial service, mentorship, and brotherhood.

The historical foundation of the Sergeant First Class formation draws upon the immense responsibilities carried by the senior sergeants and seasoned non-knightly brethren of the original Templar Order. It examines how authentic organizational strength requires men and women of tested reliability, doctrinal steadiness, and unwavering internal discipline who carry the burdens of the Order without demanding the outward glories of higher office.

Core Principles

The Sergeant First Class program requires strict adherence to the core principles that govern the conduct, decisions, and daily lives of our advanced members. Candidates are trained to embody:

  • Truth before legend: Committing to rigorous, source-based historical analysis and preserving factual history over popular, romanticized fabrications.
  • Service before status: Embracing the principle of service before self, viewing advancement as the assumption of heavier burdens and wider stewardship, not a mechanism for elevated privilege.
  • Discipline before display: Emphasizing internal spiritual mastery, orderly administration, and obedience over outward organizational pageantry or theatrical posturing.
  • Charity before privilege: Ensuring that the resources and efforts of the individual are efficiently directed toward practical charity and supporting weaker members.
  • Leadership through accountability: Recognizing that no member is above reproach, and all are accountable to God, Scripture, and the fellowship of the Order. Leaders are held to a higher standard of visible virtue and humility.

Program Audience and Scope

The Sergeant First Class handbook functions as an advanced adult formation guide specifically designed for members who have successfully completed Sergeant-level study. These members are being prepared for greater organizational trust, steadier leadership roles, and eventual transition into the Knight Aspirant formation. It serves as the mature senior-sergeant stage that strengthens leadership reliability and spiritual readiness before entry into higher vows.

Program Standards and Legitimacy Measures

Institutional integrity is guarded through strict adherence to established program standards. The Academy utilizes signed enrollment records, direct Schoolmaster oversight, written course standards, formal rank boards, and an annual curriculum review to maintain the highest quality of formation. Reading requirements and mandatory service work are integrated directly into the curriculum.

The Templar Forge Academy enforces a clear and absolute rejection of false lineage claims, invented authority, theatrical succession claims, and any unofficial military style. We are educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose. Any attempt to militarize the Order or use ranks for anything other than organizational and instructional purposes is strictly prohibited.

Leadership Expectations and Records Maintained

Candidates for Sergeant First Class are expected to maintain the highest standards of rigorous participation and selfless mentorship. To ensure these standards are met, the Academy requires the meticulous maintenance of the following records:

  • Attendance logs
  • Reading logs
  • Service logs
  • Assessment sheets
  • Oral-board notes
  • Advancement recommendations
  • Mentorship notes
  • Conduct reviews
  • Readiness observations

Assessment Model

Candidates for Sergeant First Class are continually evaluated based on five foundational pillars of assessment:

  • Knowledge: Deep comprehension of source-based Templar history, organizational rules, and doctrinal soundness.
  • Judgment: The exercise of tested Christian discernment in handling complaints, guiding newer members, and demonstrating personal self-government.
  • Service: Proven capability in organizing practical charity, supporting fellowship, and fulfilling assigned duties with punctuality and faithfulness.
  • Character: Unwavering demonstration of visible humility, the ability to accept and give correction gracefully, and profound moral integrity.
  • Communication: The ability to articulate truthful historical and doctrinal concepts clearly, honestly, and without reliance on myth or legend.

Full 8-Week Sergeant First Class Curriculum

Week 1: From Sergeant to Sergeant First Class

Primary Focus

Transition from general participation to tested reliability, senior enlisted-style responsibility, and steadiness in service.

Key Formation Subjects

Defining senior reliability; shifting from being managed to managing oneself and assisting the command structure.

Formation Emphasis

Internalizing the mandate that advanced rank means greater dependability and less need for external motivation.

Readiness Goal

Clearly articulate the shift in responsibility and the expectation of steadfast reliability in all Order operations.

Week 2: Rule, Conduct, and Visible Example

Primary Focus

Conduct standards, visible humility, consistent obedience, speech, and living as an example before newer members.

Key Formation Subjects

The Latin Rule applied to modern conduct; the power of controlled speech; the danger of pride in rank.

Formation Emphasis

Cultivating a life that naturally mentors Levies and Corporals simply through visible, humble obedience.

Readiness Goal

Demonstrate a clear understanding of how personal conduct and speech directly impact the spiritual health of the chapter.

Week 3: Historical Integrity and Doctrinal Soundness

Primary Focus

Truth before legend, factual history, rejection of false Templar myths, and doctrinal steadiness in teaching and conversation.

Key Formation Subjects

Identifying and addressing common Templar conspiracies; grounding conversations in verified source material and orthodox Christian doctrine.

Formation Emphasis

Submission to truth over sensationalism; protecting the intellectual and spiritual core of the Academy in daily interactions.

Readiness Goal

Successfully identify false historical claims and correct them using factual history and doctrinal firmness.

Week 4: Service Leadership and Brotherhood

Primary Focus

Organizing service, strengthening fellowship, practical charity, reliability, and support of weaker members.

Key Formation Subjects

Logistics of local charity; identifying brethren in need; fostering genuine unity rather than superficial association.

Formation Emphasis

Translating the virtue of charity into sustained, reliable action for both the public and internal brotherhood.

Readiness Goal

Draft a practical plan for organizing a local service initiative and supporting members who are struggling.

Week 5: Discipline, Accountability, and Correction

Primary Focus

Accepting correction, giving correction with humility, complaint handling, documented accountability, and personal self-government.

Key Formation Subjects

The biblical model of correction (Matthew 18); receiving reprimand without defense; documenting accountability fairly.

Formation Emphasis

Practicing severe personal self-government to earn the right to guide others.

Readiness Goal

Demonstrate proficiency in receiving correction gracefully and issuing gentle, documented correction to a peer or junior member.

Week 6: Stewardship, Trust, and Administrative Faithfulness

Primary Focus

Entrusted duties, attendance, punctuality, reading completion, reporting, and faithful completion of assigned responsibilities.

Key Formation Subjects

The spiritual value of administrative order; punctuality as a form of respect; thoroughness in reporting and reading logs.

Formation Emphasis

Embracing the truth that faithfulness in small administrative matters qualifies one for greater spiritual burdens.

Readiness Goal

Present a flawless record of attendance, reading logs, and assigned reports for the duration of the formation cycle.

Week 7: Spiritual Maturity and Readiness for Knight Aspirant

Primary Focus

Prayer, Scripture, self-mastery, service before self, and inward preparation for the next formation stage.

Key Formation Subjects

Deepening the daily prayer rhythm; the concept of 'monk before warrior'; assessing personal spiritual vulnerabilities.

Formation Emphasis

Leaning heavily on Christ as the ultimate foundation in preparation for the solemn vows of knighthood.

Readiness Goal

Exhibit a disciplined daily spiritual routine and articulate personal readiness for the burdens of the Knight Aspirant phase.

Week 8: Review, Oral Readiness, and Sergeant First Class Evaluation

Primary Focus

Final review, oral readiness, service record review, conduct review, and recommendation for advancement consideration.

Key Formation Subjects

Comprehensive review of history, doctrine, and operational standards; preparing for the scrutiny of the rank board.

Formation Emphasis

Cultivating a mindset of quiet obedience, regardless of the board's decision regarding future advancement.

Readiness Goal

Complete the Sergeant First Class formation cycle, presenting all required documentation and demonstrating readiness in the oral review.

Schoolmaster Guidance

The office of Sergeant First Class is not a decorative title or an automatic reward for time served. It is a rigorous proving office of trust and reliability. Schoolmasters and reviewers evaluating candidates for this rank must look for visible humility, disciplined conduct, dependable service, truthful speech, and historical seriousness.

A Sergeant First Class must possess the ability to strengthen younger or newer members by steady example. It must be stated plainly: a man who seeks status without service, or authority without personal self-government, is fundamentally not ready for this rank, nor any higher rank. No superior can command an act violating Scripture or civil law, and our senior non-commissioned leaders must exemplify this moral bedrock.

Promotion Readiness Statement

Completion of the 8-week Sergeant First Class formation cycle is required, but it does not guarantee automatic advancement into Knight Aspirant or any higher rank. The elevation to or confirmation of this rank involves rigorous evaluation by the governing boards. This evaluation comprehensively considers coursework completion, logged attendance, active participation, practical service, disciplined conduct, mentoring reliability, spiritual maturity, and overall conduct becoming of a member.

It is an inviolable rule of the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ that ranks cannot be bought or sold. Authority and progression are earned exclusively through demonstrated virtue, unwavering competence, and tested faithfulness.

Suggested Annual Academy Cycle

To sustain the health of the Academy and the continuing execution of its missions, the Sergeant First Class program operates within a strategic annual academy cycle:

  • Two major eight-week terms dedicated to classroom instruction and formation.
  • One review and oral-preparation term to ensure historical and doctrinal fidelity.
  • One service leadership campaign focused on practical, coordinated charity.
  • One mentorship and fellowship cycle to strengthen bonds across all lower ranks.
  • One recognition ceremony to honor faithful service and affirm readiness.
