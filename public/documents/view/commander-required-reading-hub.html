Syllabus Summary

Commander School is described as the transition from regional steward to institutional guardian: a man who can preserve mission, align senior officers, direct the life of the Order below the Grandmaster, govern through clear doctrine and disciplined authority, and prepare the next generation of leaders without making the office about himself. The historical model named in the syllabus is drawn from the Marshal and the Seneschal, presented as the clearest foundation for Commander-level formation in operational authority and executive stewardship.

Institutional Stewardship

Responsibility for mission, standards, leaders, and continuity across the whole Order.

Leadership Under Authority

Exercise broad authority while remaining aligned with mission, law, and higher purpose.

Leader Development

Direct, evaluate, and strengthen majors, captains, and the officer pipeline.

Promotion Readiness

Show readiness for the highest officer office beneath the Grandmaster.

Evaluation and Board Review

The syllabus evaluates attendance and participation, written assignments, command and planning work, leader-development work, the senior-command practicum, senior observation, final portfolio, and the oral board. It also requires one approved practicum such as conducting an Order-wide command assessment, leading a senior officer alignment cycle, designing a succession framework, resolving a simulated institutional crisis, or building a doctrine, communication, and morale strategy for the whole Order.

Attendance and Participation

10% — presence, preparedness, engagement, contribution to discussion.

Written Assignments

20% — historical understanding, reflection quality, doctrinal clarity, strategic application.

Senior Command Practicum

20% — judgment, alignment, communication, decision-making, and follow-through.

Oral Board / Final Review

10% — officer presence, judgment, historical grounding, readiness for highest officer office.

Sample Oral Board Questions
  • Why were the Marshal and Seneschal essential to the historical Templar Order?
  • What distinguishes a Commander from a Major in your Order?
  • Why are the Marshal and Seneschal the right models for Commander School?
  • What must a Commander protect in the Order besides outward success or activity?
  • How does a Commander exercise strong authority without becoming overbearing or centralized in the wrong way?
  • What principles should govern a Commander during a serious internal or external crisis?
  • How do you correct failing leadership while preserving legitimacy and fraternity?
  • How do you know whether the Order is actually producing future Commanders?
  • Where is your greatest weakness as a future Commander, and how are you addressing it?
  • Why are you ready—or not yet ready—for the highest officer position before Grandmaster?
