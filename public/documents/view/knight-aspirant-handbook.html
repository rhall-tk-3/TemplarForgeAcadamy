1. Purpose of the Academy and the Knight Aspirant Program

This handbook establishes the curriculum and operational guidelines for the Knight Aspirant program of the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ — Templar Forge Academy. The Academy is strictly educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose. All internal ranks, including that of Knight, are organizational and instructional only.

The Knight Aspirant program is an advanced adult formation program designed for members who have successfully completed the Sergeant First Class program. It represents a profound transition in purpose and identity — from student of history to a man committed to living the virtues of Christian knighthood in daily life.

The program is built on the foundational conviction that a true Templar Knight must first become a monk before he can rightly stand as a warrior. No man advances to knighthood on the strength of outward performance alone. The Knight Aspirant is called to cultivate genuine inward holiness, self-mastery, and a life of visible Christian virtue.

2. Distinction Between Class Phase and Essay Phase

The 8-week Knight Aspirant program is divided into two distinct phases:

  • Class Phase (Weeks 1–4) — Four weeks of active formation covering the spiritual, moral, and relational foundations of Christian knighthood. The Aspirant studies the theology of vocation, the discipline of self-mastery, the practice of active charity, and the exercise of mature judgment.
  • Essay Phase (Weeks 5–8) — Four weeks dedicated to the development, drafting, revision, and final submission of the Knight Aspirant Essay. This is a formal written expression of the Aspirant’s personal conviction, calling, and readiness for knighthood.

Both phases are of equal weight and importance. The essay cannot be divorced from the formation — it is the culminating expression of what the first four weeks have built in the man.

3. Core Principles of the Knight Aspirant

The Knight Aspirant is held to the following non-negotiable principles throughout the program and beyond:

  • Monk before warrior — Inward holiness, prayer, and submission to Christ precede any outward expression of knighthood.
  • Service before title — The rank of Knight is a call to heavier burdens, not greater privileges. Advancement is earned through faithful service.
  • Charity before display — The works of a Knight must be directed toward those in genuine need, not toward personal recognition or organizational pageantry.
  • Discipline before recognition — Internal self-mastery, accountability, and humility precede any external recognition of rank.
  • Truth before legend — All historical study is grounded in source-based, rigorous scholarship, rejecting myth, fabrication, and romantic embellishment.

4. Program Audience and Scope

The Knight Aspirant program is an 8-week adult formation program specifically designed for members who have successfully completed the Sergeant First Class program. Candidates must enter the program with a foundation of historical knowledge, organizational commitment, and demonstrated Christian character.

The program prepares the Aspirant for evaluation by the Board of Promotion, which will assess the whole man — not merely academic performance, but character, humility, conduct, and genuine readiness for the responsibilities of knighthood.

5. Program Standards and Legitimacy Measures

Institutional integrity is guarded through strict adherence to established program standards. The Academy utilizes signed enrollment records, direct Schoolmaster oversight, written course standards, formal rank boards, and an annual curriculum review to maintain the highest quality of formation.

The Templar Forge Academy enforces a clear and absolute rejection of false lineage claims, invented authority, theatrical succession claims, and any unofficial military style. The Order is educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose, and not military or paramilitary in nature.

6. Student Expectations and Records Maintained

Knight Aspirants are expected to maintain the highest standards of participation, study, daily formation, and selfless service. The Academy requires meticulous maintenance of the following records:

Attendance Logs
Reading Logs
Service Logs
Prayer & Formation Logs
Essay Progress Records
Advancement Recommendations

7. Assessment Model

Throughout the 8-week program, candidates are evaluated based on five foundational pillars of assessment:

Pillar Description
Holiness Demonstrated daily prayer, Scripture reading, and a submitted walk with Christ.
Self-Mastery Visible discipline over speech, emotion, impulse, and conduct.
Service Active, documented participation in charitable works and selfless ministry.
Character Consistent demonstration of humility, brotherhood, integrity, and moral courage.
Essay A well-crafted, honest, and substantive written expression of personal conviction and calling.

8. Program Overview

The Knight Aspirant program advances members from historical study into a season of intentional Christian formation. Through four weeks of class and four weeks of essay development, the Aspirant prepares to stand before the Board of Promotion as a man of proven character, inward discipline, and genuine conviction.

9. Full 8-Week Knight Aspirant Curriculum

Week 1
Class Phase
The Knight Aspirant Calling — Monk Before Warrior

Primary Focus

Inward formation, the necessity of daily prayer, immersion in Scripture, and the embrace of humility. Outward titles are hollow without an inward devotion to Christ and obedience to His Word.

Formation Emphasis

Recognizing that Christian knighthood begins entirely with surrender to Christ, and that the heart of a monk must come before the mantle of a warrior.

Readiness Goal

Establish a dedicated, unbroken daily rhythm of prayer and Scripture reading.

Key Emphasis

  • Monk before warrior
  • Prayerful obedience
  • Rule, order, and humility
  • Seriousness of vocation

Reflection Questions

  • In what areas of your life do you need to cultivate the “monk” (inward discipline) before you can act as the “warrior” (outward action)?
  • How does total surrender to Christ change your understanding of knighthood?

Scripture Anchors: Micah 6:8 — Matthew 5:9

Week 2
Class Phase
Spiritual Warfare, Self-Mastery, and Christian Conduct

Primary Focus

Putting on the full armor of God; mastery over speech, anger, impulse, and vanity; distinguishing between righteous zeal and reckless behavior.

Formation Emphasis

The hardest battles are fought within. True spiritual warfare means conquering one’s own ego and worldly desires.

Readiness Goal

Demonstrate visible restraint in speech and emotional response during conflicts or disagreements.

Key Emphasis

  • Full armor of God (Eph. 6)
  • Speech and anger mastery
  • Righteous zeal vs. reckless behavior
  • Inward conquest

Reflection Questions

  • When are you most tempted to respond with reckless emotion rather than disciplined restraint?
  • Which piece of the Armor of God (Ephesians 6) do you feel you lack the most right now, and why?

Scripture Anchors: Ephesians 6:10–18 — 2 Corinthians 10:3–5 — James 1:19–20

Week 3
Class Phase
Charity, Brotherhood, Service, and Witness in Modern Society

Primary Focus

Practicing mercy, selfless service, active fellowship, protection of the weak, and maintaining a Christian witness in secular society.

Formation Emphasis

Templar virtues must transfer into ordinary, daily life — at home, at work, and in the community.

Readiness Goal

Identify and execute a specific act of anonymous charity or selfless service this week.

Key Emphasis

  • Anonymous charity
  • Brotherhood and fellowship
  • Protection of the weak
  • Christian witness in daily life

Reflection Questions

  • How can you practically protect the weak or serve the marginalized in your current modern context?
  • Is your daily conduct at work and home a true reflection of the Christian witness you profess?

Scripture Anchors: Micah 6:8 — Romans 12:18 — Matthew 5:9

Week 4
Class Phase
Knightly Responsibility, Discernment, and Readiness

Primary Focus

Exercising mature judgment, knowing when to stand firm versus when to withdraw, honoring authority, and rejecting ego-driven conflict.

Formation Emphasis

True strength is often found in restraint. A Knight must discern the difference between a battle worth fighting and a distraction meant to derail them.

Readiness Goal

Prepare mentally and spiritually to begin drafting the Knight Aspirant essay.

Key Emphasis

  • Mature judgment and discernment
  • When to stand firm vs. withdraw
  • Honoring authority
  • Spiritual and essay readiness

Reflection Questions

  • Can you recall a time when withdrawing or remaining silent took more courage than fighting or arguing?
  • Are you spiritually and emotionally ready to bear the weight of Knightly responsibility?

Scripture Anchors: Romans 12:18 — Proverbs 15:1 — Ecclesiastes 3:8

Week 5
Essay Phase
Essay Planning and Outline

Primary Focus

Review the essay specifications and draft a comprehensive outline covering spiritual calling, the “monk before warrior” ethos, Templar virtues, and personal readiness.

Formation Emphasis

Clarity of thought reflects clarity of calling. Organizing the essay is an exercise in organizing one’s spiritual priorities.

Readiness Goal

Complete a structured, detailed outline for the essay.

Key Emphasis

  • Essay title: “What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?”
  • Outline: calling, monk before warrior, virtues, readiness
  • College essay format with cover page
  • Single-spaced, 2 typed pages or 1 handwritten front-and-back

Reflection Questions

  • What is the single most important conviction you want to convey in your essay?

Scripture Anchors: 1 Timothy 4:7–8 — 2 Timothy 2:3–4

Week 6
Essay Phase
First Draft and Personal Conviction

Primary Focus

Write the rough draft of the essay, meeting the length requirements (one page front and back if handwritten, or two full typed pages).

Formation Emphasis

Vulnerability and honesty before God. Do not write what sounds impressive; write what is true to your calling.

Readiness Goal

Complete the first full draft of the essay.

Key Emphasis

  • Complete rough draft
  • Honesty over impression
  • Personal conviction and calling
  • Length: 2 typed pages or 1 front-and-back handwritten

Reflection Questions

  • Does your draft reflect a desire to serve Christ, or a desire for a title?

Scripture Anchors: Ephesians 6:10–18 — Micah 6:8

Week 7
Essay Phase
Revision, Clarity, and Alignment with Templar Virtues

Primary Focus

Edit the essay for grammar, flow, and formatting. Ensure the cover page is correct, the document is single-spaced, and the essay clearly articulates the “monk before warrior” concept.

Formation Emphasis

Discipline in the small things. Presenting a neat, well-edited document demonstrates respect for the Order and the process.

Readiness Goal

Finalize the essay draft, completely free of errors.

Key Emphasis

  • Grammar, flow, and formatting reviewed
  • Cover page and single-spacing verified
  • “Monk before warrior” clearly expressed
  • Zero errors before final submission

Reflection Questions

  • How does the discipline of revising your work mirror the discipline of spiritual repentance?

Scripture Anchors: Proverbs 15:1 — Romans 12:18

Week 8
Essay Phase
Final Submission, Oral Readiness, and Review

Primary Focus

Submit the final essay by the assigned deadline and prepare to orally articulate the core points of the essay to leadership if asked.

Formation Emphasis

Standing ready. The aspirant has done the work and now submits to the judgment and timing of the Order’s leadership.

Readiness Goal

Submit the final essay and maintain impeccable conduct during the review period.

Key Emphasis

  • Final essay submitted on deadline
  • Oral articulation readiness
  • Impeccable conduct during review period
  • Yield to the Board of Promotion

Reflection Questions

  • Are you at peace with yielding your advancement entirely to the discernment of the Board of Promotion?

Scripture Anchors: 1 Timothy 4:7–8 — Micah 6:8

10. Essay Specifications

Title: “What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?”

Format: College essay format with a formal cover page. Cover page must include full name, date, and program title.

Length: Two full typed pages (single-spaced) or one handwritten page (front and back, full). No significant blank space.

Submission: Submitted digitally through the Academy portal or in person to the Schoolmaster by the assigned deadline (Week 8).

Content: The essay must reflect the Aspirant’s personal spiritual calling, their understanding of the “monk before warrior” principle, which Templar virtues they intend to live, and their personal readiness for the responsibilities of knighthood.

11. Schoolmaster Guidance

Schoolmasters bear the burden of ensuring that Knight Aspirants meet the rigorous demands of this program. Candidates must complete the 8-week program in its entirety, remain current with assigned classwork and readings, maintain regular attendance at meetings, Bible studies, and Round Tables, and demonstrate active fellowship and participation in the daily life of the Order.

Advancement must be based on demonstrated faithfulness, discipline, participation, and completion of all requirements — including the essay. Deficiencies in conduct, coursework, or character may delay promotion, subject to Board review. Schoolmasters must emphasize genuine holiness, active charitable works, disciplined character, and visible humility above all else.

12. Promotion Readiness Statement

Completion of the 8-week Knight Aspirant curriculum is required but does not guarantee automatic promotion. The Board of Promotion Review will conduct a holistic evaluation of the candidate. This evaluation considers coursework completion, essay quality, logged attendance, active participation, committed service, disciplined conduct, and overall character becoming of a Knight.

It is an unyielding principle of the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ that ranks cannot be bought or sold. Advancement is earned solely through merit, character, and an ongoing dedication to the charitable and educational mission of the Order.

13. Suggested Annual Academy Cycle

To provide structure and consistency for adult formation, the Knight Aspirant program operates on a suggested annual cycle:

  1. One or two major eight-week terms for advancing Sergeant First Class graduates.
  2. One review term dedicated to formation deepening, remediation, and fellowship.
  3. One chapter lecture series bringing in advanced historical and spiritual topics.
  4. One annual service campaign where Knight Aspirants take active operational roles in charitable work.
  5. One recognition ceremony to formally elevate those who have met the standards of the Board of Promotion.

14. Closing Affirmation

The Knight Aspirant Program of the Templar Forge Academy stands as a testament to our commitment to forming men of genuine Christian virtue, historical integrity, and sacrificial service. We remain an organization that is strictly educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose, forever consecrated to Jesus Christ.

The aspirant who completes this program and stands before the Board of Promotion does so not as one who has mastered outward performance, but as one who has cultivated the heart of a monk, the discipline of a servant, and the humility of a man who knows his calling comes from God alone — not from any title conferred by men.

We affirm that service before self is our highest calling, and we declare unconditionally that no superior can command an act violating Scripture or civil law. Those who bear the title of Knight are held to a higher standard of visible virtue and humility, ensuring that our Order remains a beacon of nonviolent, truthful witness to the Kingdom of Christ.
