Templar Forge Academy — 8-Week Sergeant Formation Program

Purpose of the Academy and Sergeant Program

This handbook establishes the curriculum and operational guidelines for the Sergeant program of the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ — Templar Forge Academy. The Academy is strictly educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose. The Sergeant program serves as a critical phase of adult formation, requiring candidates to move beyond introductory frameworks and assume greater responsibility in historical understanding and organizational stewardship.

It must be explicitly stated that the Order and its Academy are not military or paramilitary in nature. All internal ranks, including that of Sergeant, are organizational and instructional only. The program exists to forge individuals of strong moral character dedicated to charitable works and rigorous intellectual formation, completely devoid of martial posturing.

Mission and Historical Foundation

The primary mission of the Templar Forge Academy is to cultivate a truthful understanding of the medieval Knights Templar, rejecting conjecture and myth in favor of rigorous, source-based history. The Order is forever consecrated to Jesus Christ, and this dedication shapes our modern mission to provide a truthful, nonviolent witness to His gospel through sacrificial service and brotherhood.

The historical foundation of the Sergeant program delves deeply into the complex realities of the Order's expansion, hierarchy, and logistical brilliance. We examine their administrative structure, the burden of their logistics, the strict discipline of the Latin Rule, and the vulnerabilities brought on by vast wealth and political entanglement.

Core Principles

The Sergeant program requires strict adherence to the core principles that govern the conduct, study, and daily lives of our members. Candidates are trained to embody:

  • Truth before legend — Committing to rigorous, source-based historical analysis over popular, romanticized fabrications.
  • Service before status — Embracing the principle of service before self, viewing advancement as a call to heavier burdens, not greater privileges.
  • Discipline before display — Emphasizing internal spiritual mastery, strict conduct, and obedience over outward organizational pageantry.
  • Charity before privilege — Ensuring that the resources, influence, and efforts of the Order are directed toward those in genuine need.
  • Leadership through accountability — Recognizing that no member is above reproach, and all are accountable to God, Scripture, and the fellowship of the Order.

Program Audience and Scope

The Sergeant program is an 8-week adult formation program specifically designed for members who have successfully completed the Corporal program. It transitions the candidate from a foundational study of the Order's history into a rigorous, source-based analysis of the medieval Knights Templar's operations, logistics, and discipline, preparing the candidate for mature organizational leadership.

Program Standards and Legitimacy Measures

Institutional integrity is guarded through strict adherence to established program standards. The Academy utilizes signed enrollment records, direct Schoolmaster oversight, written course standards, formal rank boards, and an annual curriculum review to maintain the highest quality of formation. Reading requirements and mandatory service work are integrated directly into the curriculum.

The Templar Forge Academy enforces a clear and absolute rejection of false lineage claims, invented authority, theatrical succession claims, and any unofficial military style. The Order is educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose, and not military or paramilitary in nature. Internal ranks are organizational and instructional only.

Student Expectations and Records Maintained

Candidates in the Sergeant program are expected to maintain the highest standards of participation, rigorous study, and selfless service. To ensure these standards are met, the Academy requires meticulous maintenance of the following records:

Assessment Model

Throughout the 8-week program, candidates are evaluated based on five foundational pillars of assessment:

Pillar Description
Knowledge Mastery of source-based history, organizational structure, and logistics.
Judgment The exercise of mature Christian discernment and critical thinking in historical analysis.
Service Active, documented participation in the charitable works and stewardship of the Order.
Character Consistent demonstration of rigorous discipline, brotherhood, and moral integrity.
Communication The ability to articulate the history and complex principles of the Order effectively and truthfully.

Program Overview

The Sergeant program advances adult members from foundational study into source-based historical analysis, organization, logistics, stewardship, disciplined service, and accountable leadership. Through this rigorous 8-week study, candidates prepare to bear the practical burdens of the Order.

Full 8-Week Sergeant Curriculum

Week 1
From Corporal to Sergeant — Historical Review, Scope, and Source-Based Method

Primary Focus

Transition from Corporal foundations into critical, source-based history and disciplined study methods.

Key Historical Subjects

Primary vs. secondary historical sources, historiography of the Templars, and critical review of initial lore.

Formation Emphasis

Cultivating intellectual rigor, analytical discipline, and an unwavering commitment to objective truth.

Readiness Goal

Demonstrate the ability to analyze historical texts and distinguish source-based fact from historical fiction.

Week 2
Expansion, Patronage, and Legitimacy in Europe

Primary Focus

European expansion, noble patronage, papal privileges, and institutional legitimacy.

Key Historical Subjects

The papal bull Omne Datum Optimum, acquisition of European estates, and the network of aristocratic support.

Formation Emphasis

Understanding how legitimate authority and strategic stewardship are required to sustain a charitable mission.

Readiness Goal

Map the expansion of the Order's influence across Europe and explain the impact of papal privileges.

Week 3
Organization, Offices, Provinces, and Internal Classes

Primary Focus

Hierarchy, provinces, preceptories, internal classes, and administrative structure.

Key Historical Subjects

The division of provinces, the roles of local Preceptors and Priors, and the distinction between internal classes.

Formation Emphasis

Valuing organizational structure, clear communication, and the necessity of accountable delegation.

Readiness Goal

Accurately outline the provincial hierarchy and administrative network of the medieval Knights Templar.

Week 4
Military Operations, Logistics, Command, and Disciplined Service

Primary Focus

Castle logistics, command structure, campaign support, disciplined service, and the difference between historical military study and modern non-military identity.

Key Historical Subjects

Supply chains, the maintenance of Crusader castles, strategic command, and the logistical burden of the Holy Land.

Formation Emphasis

Recognizing that effective service relies on unseen logistical support and disciplined preparation, not just outward action.

Readiness Goal

Analyze the logistical operations of the Order while clearly articulating why the modern Order rejects paramilitary styling.

Week 5
Finance, Stewardship, Administration, and Public Trust

Primary Focus

Treasury, transport, stewardship, ethical dangers of wealth, and public confidence.

Key Historical Subjects

The Templars as medieval bankers, the safe transport of funds, and the management of vast European treasuries.

Formation Emphasis

The moral weight of financial stewardship, transparency, and the profound spiritual dangers associated with wealth.

Readiness Goal

Explain the Order's financial mechanisms and articulate the modern ethical standards required of a Sergeant.

Week 6
Rule, Discipline, Conduct, and Brotherhood

Primary Focus

The Latin Rule, internal discipline, spiritual accountability, brotherhood, and conduct standards.

Key Historical Subjects

The specific chapters of the Latin Rule, methods of internal correction, penance, and the daily life of the brethren.

Formation Emphasis

Submission to communal discipline, the correction of peers in love, and the forging of deep Christian brotherhood.

Readiness Goal

Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the Latin Rule's approach to discipline and accountability.

Week 7
Decline, Political Pressure, and Institutional Vulnerability

Primary Focus

Political exposure, reputation risk, suppression pressures, and vulnerability created by wealth and influence.

Key Historical Subjects

The shift in public perception, the loss of the Holy Land (1291), and the growing tension with secular monarchs.

Formation Emphasis

The inevitability of worldly hostility, managing institutional vulnerability, and leading faithfully under pressure.

Readiness Goal

Evaluate the socio-political factors that made the Templars vulnerable to the machinations of the French Crown.

Week 8
Legacy, Judgment, and Sergeant Readiness Review

Primary Focus

Full review, historical lessons, accountability, oral review, and readiness for Sergeant promotion evaluation.

Key Historical Subjects

Comprehensive review of source-based history, logistics, and the administrative legacy of the Order.

Formation Emphasis

Consolidating historical knowledge with moral judgment to prepare for accountable leadership within the Order.

Readiness Goal

Demonstrate absolute readiness for the Sergeant oral review and promotion evaluation board.

Schoolmaster Guidance

Schoolmasters bear the burden of ensuring that candidates for Sergeant meet the rigorous demands of this program. Candidates must complete the 8-week class in its entirety, remain current with assigned classwork and readings, maintain regular attendance at meetings, Bible studies, and Round Tables, and demonstrate active fellowship and participation in the daily life of the Order.

Advancement must be based on demonstrated faithfulness, discipline, participation, and completion of all requirements. Deficiencies in conduct or coursework may delay promotion, subject to Board review. Schoolmasters must emphasize factual history, active charitable works, disciplined character, and visible humility above all else.

Promotion Readiness Statement

Completion of the 8-week Sergeant curriculum is required but does not guarantee automatic promotion. The Board of Promotion Review will conduct a holistic evaluation of the candidate. This evaluation considers coursework completion, logged attendance, active participation, committed service, disciplined conduct, and overall conduct becoming of a member.

It is an unyielding principle of the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ that ranks cannot be bought or sold. Advancement is earned solely through merit, character, and an ongoing dedication to the charitable and educational mission of the Order.

Suggested Annual Academy Cycle

To provide structure and consistency for adult formation, the Sergeant program operates on a suggested annual cycle:

  1. Two major eight-week terms for advancing Corporal graduates.
  2. One review term dedicated to remediation, deep historical study, and fellowship.
  3. One chapter lecture series bringing in advanced topics and guest instruction.
  4. One annual service campaign where Sergeants are expected to take active operational roles.
  5. One recognition ceremony to formally elevate those who have met the standards of the Board.
