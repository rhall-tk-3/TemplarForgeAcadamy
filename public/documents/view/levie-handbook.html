Templar Forge Academy — Introductory Formation Program

Purpose of the Academy and Levie Program

This handbook establishes the formal schooling system for the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ - Templar Forge Academy. The Academy is strictly educational, charitable, historical, and moral in purpose. The Levie program serves as the entry point for all new members, designed to impart a foundational understanding of the Order's principles, history, and expectations.

It must be explicitly stated that the Order and its Academy are not military or paramilitary in nature; all internal ranks are organizational and instructional only. The aim is to form individuals in Christian virtue, character, and historical appreciation, rather than martial prowess.

Mission and Historical Foundation

The mission of the Templar Forge Academy is to provide a truthful understanding of the medieval Knights Templar, anchoring members in factual history rather than popular myth. The Order is forever consecrated to Jesus Christ, and its primary modern mission is to provide a truthful, nonviolent witness to His gospel through charitable service and brotherhood.

The historical foundation of the Academy focuses on the true arc of the medieval Knights Templar—from the context of the First Crusade and the necessity of protecting pilgrims, through their formal establishment at the Council of Troyes, to their eventual suppression.

Core Principles

Every level of the Templar Forge Academy, beginning with the Levie program, operates upon strict core principles that dictate the conduct and mindset of its members. The Academy trains members to value:

  • Truth before legend: Relying on factual, historical study rather than romanticized myths.
  • Service before status: Viewing participation as an opportunity to serve others rather than elevate oneself.
  • Discipline before display: Prioritizing inward character and self-control over outward symbols.
  • Charity before privilege: Utilizing the organization to give freely to those in need.
  • Leadership through accountability: Ensuring that all members, regardless of organizational rank, remain accountable to God and each other.

Program Audience and Scope

The Levie program is specifically designed as the entry-level formation and probationary period for new members of the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ. It spans a mandatory 60-day (8-week) probationary term. During this time, the candidate, known as a Levie, is expected to familiarize themselves with the Order's rules, history, and moral expectations before full admittance is considered.

Program Standards and Legitimacy Measures

To ensure institutional integrity and proper formation, the Academy enforces strict legitimacy measures. The program utilizes signed enrollment records, oversight by the Schoolmaster office, written course standards, formal rank boards, and an annual curriculum review.

The Academy strictly rejects false lineage claims, invented authority, theatrical succession claims, or unofficial military styling. Safeguarding and oversight protocols are implemented throughout all instruction. The curriculum incorporates mandatory reading requirements and mandatory service work to demonstrate active Christian charity.

Reading Log Requirement

Consistent study is a primary metric for determining readiness. Every Levie must maintain an official Reading Log. This log serves as definitive proof of coursework completion and active participation throughout the 8-week cycle. Deficiencies in the Reading Log, including failure to complete the required readings during the probationary period, will directly delay or prevent advancement.

Student Expectations and Records Maintained

Students are expected to attend all scheduled meetings, complete assigned readings, and engage in charitable service. The Academy meticulously maintains the following records for each Levie:

  • Attendance logs
  • Reading logs
  • Service logs
  • Assessment sheets
  • Oral-board notes
  • Advancement recommendations

Assessment Model

The Academy evaluates all Levies based on five foundational pillars of assessment:

  • Knowledge: Comprehension of the rules, statutes, and factual history.
  • Judgment: The ability to apply Christian discernment and adhere to the liberty of conscience.
  • Service: Demonstrated willingness to engage in charitable works.
  • Character: Visible alignment with the moral virtues of the Order.
  • Communication: The capacity to articulate the Order's principles truthfully and respectfully.

Full 8-Week Levie Curriculum

Week 1: Identity, Purpose, and Consecration

Required Reading

Rules and Statutes Articles I-IV

Guided Reading Focus

Focus on the Christian nature of the Order, the Holy Trinity, consecration to Jesus Christ, the foundational mission, and truthful nonviolent witness.

Key Terms/Figures

Christian nature, Trinity, Consecration to Jesus Christ.

Memory Targets

The Order is forever consecrated to Jesus Christ; its mission is truthful nonviolent witness.

Readiness Goals

Demonstrate a clear understanding of the Order's spiritual identity and ultimate allegiance.

Week 2: Standards, Duties, and Equality

Required Reading

Rules and Statutes Articles IX-XII and Promotion Manual By-Laws Articles I-III

Guided Reading Focus

Understand the fundamental duties of members, the requirement of financial integrity, equality among all brothers and sisters, and mandatory attendance expectations.

Key Terms/Figures

Duties, Financial Integrity, Equality.

Memory Targets

Equality is paramount; attendance and integrity are non-negotiable requirements for standing.

Readiness Goals

Commit to the organizational duties and understand the standard of fairness within the ranks.

Week 3: Conduct, Leadership, and Accountability

Required Reading

Rules and Statutes Articles VI-VIII and XIII-XIV

Guided Reading Focus

Examine leadership standards, the absolute liberty of conscience, the distinction of open vows versus secret oaths, and the procedures for complaint and redress.

Key Terms/Figures

Leadership Standards, Liberty of Conscience, Open Vows, Complaint/Redress.

Memory Targets

No superior can command an act violating Scripture or civil law; all vows are open and public.

Readiness Goals

Understand the mechanisms of accountability and the protection of individual conscience.

Week 4: The Five Governing Pillars

Required Reading

Rules and Statutes Articles XV-XVII

Guided Reading Focus

Study the core pillars: Service, Prayer, Discipline, Witness, and Charity. Understand the nature of spiritual warfare and the call to sacrificial brotherhood/sisterhood.

Key Terms/Figures

Service, Prayer, Discipline, Witness, Charity, Spiritual Warfare.

Memory Targets

Memorize the Five Governing Pillars and define sacrificial brotherhood/sisterhood.

Readiness Goals

Internalize the foundational daily practices required of all members of the Order.

Week 5: The Need for Protection

Required Reading

Levie History Handout 1

Guided Reading Focus

Analyze the historical context of Christian pilgrimage, the First Crusade, the geographical importance of Jerusalem, and the extreme danger to travelers during the 11th and 12th centuries.

Key Terms/Figures

Christian Pilgrimage, First Crusade, Jerusalem.

Memory Targets

The early roads to Jerusalem were perilous, necessitating organized protection for the faithful.

Readiness Goals

Accurately describe the historical conditions that necessitated the formation of the Templars.

Week 6: Founding People and Places

Required Reading

Levie History Handout 2

Guided Reading Focus

Learn about Hugh de Payns, the support of King Baldwin II, the allocation of the Temple Mount, and the explicit founding purpose of protecting pilgrims.

Key Terms/Figures

Hugh de Payns, King Baldwin II, Temple Mount.

Memory Targets

Hugh de Payns founded the Order to protect pilgrims, headquartered at the Temple Mount.

Readiness Goals

Identify the founding figures and articulate the original historical mission of the Knights Templar.

Week 7: Official Approval and Early Support

Required Reading

Levie History Handout 3

Guided Reading Focus

Examine the Council of Troyes, the advocacy of Bernard of Clairvaux, and how the Order gained formal approval and institutional legitimacy within the Church.

Key Terms/Figures

Council of Troyes, Bernard of Clairvaux.

Memory Targets

The Council of Troyes granted formal ecclesiastical approval and established the Latin Rule.

Readiness Goals

Explain how a small band of knights achieved international recognition and legitimacy.

Week 8: Religious-Order Identity and Early Timeline Review

Required Reading

Levie History Handout 4

Guided Reading Focus

Review the monastic elements of the Order—prayer and discipline. Conduct a comprehensive review from 1095 to the Council of Troyes to prepare for the final evaluation.

Key Terms/Figures

Prayer, Discipline, 1095 to Troyes Timeline.

Memory Targets

The Templars were a religious order of monks bound by discipline, not merely warriors.

Readiness Goals

Demonstrate readiness for the final written quiz and oral evaluation.

  • Schoolmaster Guidance

Schoolmasters bear the solemn responsibility of guiding the Levie through this probationary period. It is mandatory to conduct weekly verification of the official Reading Logs and maintain strictly accurate attendance records. Schoolmasters must facilitate moderated discussions that heavily emphasize honesty, spiritual identity, and historical truth.

Under no circumstances should instruction glorify violence or spread unverified legends regarding the Templars. Where appropriate, particularly in age-appropriate discussions involving younger participants engaging with the material, parental and instructor oversight language must be utilized to maintain a safe, educational environment.

Promotion Readiness Statement

Completion of the 8-week curriculum is a strict requirement for advancement, but it does not guarantee automatic promotion. At the conclusion of the 60-day probationary period, the Board of Promotion Review evaluates each Levie. This evaluation is based upon absolute coursework completion, logged attendance, active participation, and demonstrated conduct becoming of a member of the Order.

The Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ operates on merit and spiritual character. Ranks cannot be bought or sold under any circumstances. Promotion is a recognition of service, humility, and readiness to assume greater organizational responsibilities.

Suggested Annual Academy Cycle

To maintain consistency and proper administration of the Levie program, the Templar Forge Academy follows a structured annual cycle. This includes:

  • Two major eight-week terms for new Levie cohorts.
  • One summer review program for remediation and continued study.
  • One chapter lecture series focusing on broader historical and spiritual topics.
  • One annual service campaign requiring mandatory participation from all ranks.
  • One school banquet or recognition ceremony to formally acknowledge those who have successfully passed the Board of Promotion Review.

Closing Affirmation

The Levie Program of the Templar Forge Academy exists to lay a foundation of unshakeable integrity. Let it be known and affirmed by all who enter this Order that no superior

can command an act violating Scripture or civil law. Those who hold instructional or organizational rank are not masters to be served, but rather leaders held to a higher standard of visible virtue and humility. As we study the past, we remain forever consecrated to Jesus Christ, committed solely to a truthful, nonviolent witness in the modern world.
