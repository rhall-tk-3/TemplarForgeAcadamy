Syllabus Summary

Lieutenant School is the transition from committed member to dependable officer: a man who can receive mission, execute it faithfully, care for those assigned to him, maintain standards, and represent the Order with humility and steadiness. The historical model named in the syllabus is the Under Marshal — the clearest junior operational officer parallel in the Templar hierarchy.

Officer Responsibility

Readiness, standards, accountability, and care of assigned brothers.

Leadership Under Authority

Receive direction, execute faithfully, report clearly, and lead without ego.

Character Formation

Humility, disciplined speech, reliability, self-control, and fraternal care.

Team Leadership

Lead a small team, assignment, meeting, or service element effectively.

Evaluation and Board Review

The syllabus evaluates attendance and participation, written assignments, readiness and stewardship work, the small-team practicum, mentoring observation, final portfolio, and the oral board. One approved practicum is required — leading a ceremonial team, organizing a service project, running a training segment, coordinating a meeting, or managing readiness for an event.

Attendance and Participation

10% — presence, preparedness, engagement.

Written Assignments

20% — history, reflection, application.

Small-Team Practicum

20% — execution, clarity, accountability, follow-through.

Oral Board / Final Review

20% — presence, judgment, historical understanding, readiness.

Sample Oral Board Questions
  • Why does a Templar-inspired officer need history?
  • Why is obedience foundational to leadership?
  • What distinguishes a Lieutenant from a senior member without office?
  • Why is the Under Marshal the best historical parallel for this rank?
  • What does it mean for a Lieutenant to care for the brothers?
  • Where is your greatest growth area as a future officer?
  • How would you respond to lateness, disorder, or poor communication in your team?
