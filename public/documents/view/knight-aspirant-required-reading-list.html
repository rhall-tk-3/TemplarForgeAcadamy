Developed progressively across Weeks 5–8 and submitted for final review with the reading record and program documentation.

Program Overview

The Knight Aspirant phase is a preparatory school of identity, discipline, prayer, virtue, and readiness. The early weeks establish the aspirant as a Christian man under rule before any claim to outward chivalric identity. The later weeks pivot to essay development, reflection, and final review. Formation at this level is measured not by enthusiasm or outward appearance but by inward change, steady discipline, and willingness to be shaped before claiming any title or honor.

The historical Templars were first monks before they were warriors, bound by strict vows, communal life, and ordered prayer. That foundation of inward formation before outward office is the lens for this entire program phase. The aspirant enters as a student of identity, not yet a claimant of titles. Britannica: Knights Templar

Week One
The Calling of a Knight Aspirant
Primary Focus

Establish the identity of the Knight Aspirant as a Christian man under discipline, learning that a Templar must first become a monk before he can rightly stand as a warrior.

Key Formation Subjects

Christian identity under rule; submission to discipline; the inward foundations of chivalric formation; understanding rank as responsibility rather than honor.

Formation Emphasis

Receiving the truth that spiritual formation must precede outward office, symbolism, or martial language.

Readiness Goal

Explain why a Knight Aspirant must first be formed as a disciplined Christian man before he can be considered ready for further advancement.

Required Reading
  • Knight Aspirant Program Handbook
  • KTKC Promotion Manual and By-Laws
  • KTKC Rules and Statutes
Historical Reference

Britannica describes the historical Templars as bound by vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience, subject to ordered prayer, communal discipline, and explicit prohibitions against gambling, swearing, and drunkenness. That combination of monastic rule and military service provides the lens for this week's formation theme: the Templar was always a monk first. Britannica: Knights Templar

Week Two
Prayer, Scripture, and Rule of Life
Primary Focus

Train the aspirant to build a daily rule of life grounded in prayer, Scripture, confession, sober speech, and steady accountability.

Key Formation Subjects

Daily prayer rhythm, Scripture intake, moral speech, confession, and practical accountability structures for Christian formation.

Formation Emphasis

Establishing stable habits that shape the aspirant from the inside rather than relying on occasional zeal or seasonal commitment.

Readiness Goal

Demonstrate understanding of a daily Christian rule of life and how it supports stability, obedience, and readiness for Knight formation.

Required Reading
  • Knight Aspirant Program Handbook
  • KTKC Rules and Statutes
  • Britannica: Knights Templar
Historical Reference

The original Rule of the Templars imposed canonical hours, regulated sleep, set standards for dress, diet, and speech, and required confession. The aspirant is not held to those exact standards, but the principle of a structured rule over daily life applies directly to this week's formation work. Britannica: Knights Templar

Week Three
Virtue, Charity, and Self-Mastery
Primary Focus

Focus on the virtues expected of a Christian Templar: charity, integrity, restraint, courage, mercy, honorable speech, and brotherly service.

Key Formation Subjects

Christian virtue, disciplined character, brotherly care, and mastery of appetite, tongue, and temper in ordinary daily life.

Formation Emphasis

Learning that maturity is proven in conduct, mercy, restraint, and service rather than outward appearance or intensity of feeling.

Readiness Goal

Identify the core virtues expected of a Knight Aspirant and describe how they should appear in ordinary chapter life and daily interaction.

Required Reading
  • Knight Aspirant Program Handbook
  • KTKC Promotion Manual and By-Laws
  • Britannica: Knights Templar
  • OSMTH: Modern Order History
Historical Reference

The Templar Rule prohibited boasting, vainglory, jealousy, and excessive talk. It required mercy toward the poor, restraint in combat without command, and humility before God and the chapter. These are formation standards, not simply ancient rules. OSMTH: Modern Order History

Week Four
Spiritual Warfare, Brotherhood, and Knight Readiness
Primary Focus

Clarify that the Knight's true warfare is first spiritual: resisting sin, guarding speech, protecting the weak, and standing faithfully within Christian brotherhood.

Key Formation Subjects

Spiritual warfare, disciplined speech, protection of the weak, and fidelity to Christian brotherhood as the foundation of knightly identity.

Formation Emphasis

Reframing knightly readiness as spiritual steadiness, moral clarity, and faithful presence among the brethren rather than outward romanticism.

Readiness Goal

Articulate the difference between outward romanticism and true Christian knightly readiness grounded in spiritual discipline and brotherly accountability.

Required Reading
  • Knight Aspirant Program Handbook
  • KTKC Promotion Manual and By-Laws
  • KTKC Rules and Statutes
Historical Reference

Bernard of Clairvaux's De Laude Novae Militiae (In Praise of the New Knighthood) drew the distinction between secular knights fighting for personal glory and the Templar knight fighting under discipline, in submission to God and chapter. That distinction between self-serving knighthood and service-oriented formation is the central theme of this week. Britannica: Knights Templar

Week Five
Essay Week I — Theme, Thesis, and Evidence
Primary Focus

Launch the required essay by identifying the central claim, spiritual theme, and supporting sources for “What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?”

Key Formation Subjects

Theme selection, thesis development, source identification, and disciplined reflection that moves from personal feeling to ordered Christian argument.

Formation Emphasis

Moving from vague aspiration to clear thought, grounded evidence, and sincere Christian reflection rather than sentimental or romantic framing.

Readiness Goal

Produce a clear essay direction with an identifiable thesis statement and an identified source base from program readings.

Required Reading
  • Knight Aspirant Required Reading List (this document)
  • Knight Aspirant Program Handbook
  • KTKC Promotion Manual and By-Laws
  • KTKC Rules and Statutes
Essay Direction Note

The essay is not a report on Templar history. It is a personal Christian reflection grounded in program readings. The thesis should make a clear claim about what the role of a Templar Knight means in terms of spiritual formation, Christian obedience, and service within the Order. Evidence should come from program documents, KTKC rules, and approved historical sources.

Week Six
Essay Week II — Outline and First Draft
Primary Focus

Build a complete outline and begin the full draft in standard college essay format, keeping the argument centered on Christian formation and personal commitment.

Key Formation Subjects

Outline structure, paragraph order, evidence integration, and disciplined drafting that moves steadily toward a complete and honest document.

Formation Emphasis

Treating writing as an act of order, honesty, and careful self-expression rather than rushed opinion or sentimental decoration.

Readiness Goal

Present a workable outline and an honest first draft that reflects the theme of Christian knightly formation in the student's own words and experience.

Required Reading
  • Knight Aspirant Required Reading List (this document)
  • Knight Aspirant Program Handbook
  • Britannica: Knights Templar
Essay Format Requirements
  • Standard college essay format
  • Cover page required (name, date, program, rank level)
  • Single-spaced body text
  • One full page front and back, or two full typed pages minimum
  • Title: “What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?”
Week Seven
Essay Week III — Revision, Clarity, and Submission Readiness
Primary Focus

Revise for clarity, strength, sincerity, and discipline so the essay reflects maturity, not haste or sentimentality.

Key Formation Subjects

Revision technique, clarity of argument, honest tone, and the removal of weak, vague, or sentimental framing that does not reflect genuine Christian formation.

Formation Emphasis

Learning that mature speech and writing require restraint, refinement, and truthful self-examination rather than impressive language or dramatic feeling.

Readiness Goal

Submit a revised draft that is orderly, clear, and fitting for final review by the schoolmaster.

Required Reading
  • Knight Aspirant Required Reading List (this document)
  • Knight Aspirant Program Handbook
  • KTKC Promotion Manual and By-Laws
Revision Checklist
  1. Thesis is clear and identifiable in the opening paragraph
  2. Evidence comes from program readings, not personal stories alone
  3. Tone is sober, honest, and grounded — not romantic or inflated
  4. Cover page is complete with all required fields
  5. Format is single-spaced, two typed pages minimum
  6. Language reflects Christian formation, not outward performance
Week Eight
Essay Week IV — Final Submission and Knight Review
Primary Focus

Submit the final essay with cover page and formatting requirements completed, then undergo final review for Knight Aspirant readiness and program completion.

Key Formation Subjects

Final compliance, submission readiness, program review standards, and disciplined completion of all required documentation.

Formation Emphasis

Finishing well, honoring requirements, and presenting oneself for review with humility and seriousness rather than pride or complaint.

Readiness Goal

Complete the Knight Aspirant formation cycle with the required essay and supporting reading record fully prepared for final review by the schoolmaster.

Required Reading

  • Knight Aspirant Program Handbook
  • OSMTH: Modern Order History
  • KTKC Promotion Manual and By-Laws
Final Submission Requirements
  1. Essay: “What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?” — final, formatted copy
