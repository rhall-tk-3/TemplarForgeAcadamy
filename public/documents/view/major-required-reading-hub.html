Syllabus Summary

Major School is described as the transition from local commander to regional steward: a man who can strengthen multiple bodies, inspect standards, develop captains, align operations across a broader structure, and preserve the health of the Order beyond one local command. The historical model named in the syllabus is the Provincial Master / Grand Prior, presented as the clearest model for wider oversight, stronger systems, and development across multiple commands.

Regional Stewardship

Responsibility for multiple bodies, shared standards, and wider health.

Leadership Under Authority

Oversee captains and local bodies in alignment with mission and higher authority.

Leader Development

Evaluate, coach, and strengthen captains and future officers through structured support.

Promotion Readiness

Show readiness for senior command through oversight, planning, and leader development.

Evaluation and Board Review

The syllabus evaluates attendance and participation, written assignments, planning and assessment tools, leader-development work, the regional oversight practicum, senior observation, final portfolio, and the oral board. It also requires one approved practicum such as conducting a structured review of local bodies, coordinating a regional officer-development effort, building a district growth and mentoring plan, leading a support initiative for a struggling command, or designing a regional accountability or communication system.

Attendance and Participation

10% — presence, preparedness, engagement, contribution to discussion.

Written Assignments

20% — historical understanding, reflection quality, strategic application.

Regional Oversight Practicum

20% — judgment, coordination, evaluation, communication, follow-through.

Oral Board / Final Review

10% — officer presence, judgment, historical grounding, readiness for Major-level office.

Sample Oral Board Questions
  • Why were Provincial Masters essential to the historical Templar Order?
  • What distinguishes a Major from a Captain in your Order?
  • Why is the Provincial Master / Grand Prior the right model for Major School?
  • What must a Major protect across multiple bodies besides outward activity?
  • How do you inspect and correct without becoming merely punitive or bureaucratic?
  • How do you know whether a Captain is healthy, stagnant, or failing?
  • What kind of speech most harms a region, and how should a Major prevent it?
  • How do you decide where limited time and support should be concentrated?
  • Where is your greatest weakness as a future Major, and how are you addressing it?
  • Why are you ready—or not yet ready—for wider oversight?
