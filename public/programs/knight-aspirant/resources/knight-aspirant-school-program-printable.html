8-Week Knight Aspirant Program

Knight Aspirant School

This program prepares the candidate for knighthood by insisting that a true Templar must first be a monk before a warrior. The class phase forms prayer, Scripture, humility, charity, and self-mastery; the essay phase tests reflection, discipline, and readiness for Knight review.

View the 8-week path Open required reading

Program anchors

  • 4 weeks of class formation
  • 4 weeks of essay development and submission
  • Required essay: What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?
  • Readiness judged by conduct, discipline, attendance, and maturity

Program overview

The Knight Aspirant season is the final bridge before promotion to Knight. It is a spiritual and moral formation process rather than a ceremonial checklist.

Total Length8 Weeks
Core ThemeMonk Before Warrior
Formation TargetVirtue & Readiness
Final RequirementEssay & Review
Promotion expectations draw from the Promotion Manual & By-Laws and KTKC Rules & Statutes. The required essay title is “What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?”, prepared in standard college format with a cover page, single spacing, and the required page length.

Phase I · Weeks 1–4

Class instruction on calling, prayer, Scripture, rule of life, virtue, charity, discipline, spiritual warfare, and Christian brotherhood.

Browse weekly lesson pages

Phase II · Weeks 5–8

Essay development, outlining, drafting, revision, final submission, and final Knight readiness review by the Schoolmaster and leadership.

Week-by-week path

A concise pathway through class instruction and essay submission.

Week 1: The Calling of a Knight Aspirant

Class Phase · Establish the identity of the Knight Aspirant as a Christian man under discipline, learning that a Templar must first become a monk before he can rightly stand as a warrior.

Week 2: Prayer, Scripture, and Rule of Life

Class Phase · Train the aspirant to build a daily rule of life grounded in prayer, Scripture, confession, sober speech, and steady accountability.

Week 3: Virtue, Charity, and Self-Mastery

Class Phase · Focus on the virtues expected of a Christian Templar: charity, integrity, restraint, courage, mercy, honorable speech, and brotherly service.

Week 4: Spiritual Warfare, Brotherhood, and Knight Readiness

Class Phase · Clarify that the Knight’s true warfare is first spiritual: resisting sin, guarding speech, protecting the weak, and standing faithfully within Christian brotherhood.

Week 5: Essay Week I — Theme, Thesis, and Evidence

Essay Phase · Launch the required essay by identifying the central claim, spiritual theme, and supporting sources for “What Does a Templar Knight Mean to You?”

Week 6: Essay Week II — Outline and First Draft

Essay Phase · Build a complete outline and begin the full draft in standard college essay format, keeping the argument centered on Christian formation.

Week 7: Essay Week III — Revision, Clarity, and Submission Readiness

Essay Phase · Revise for clarity, strength, sincerity, and discipline so the essay reflects maturity, not haste or sentimentality.

Week 8: Essay Week IV — Final Submission and Knight Review

Essay Phase · Submit the final essay with cover page and formatting requirements completed, then undergo final review for Knight readiness.

Readiness goal

Adopt the program as a season of formation rather than a formality.

Reflection questions

  • Why must inward discipline come before outward authority?
  • What habits already support or hinder your formation toward knighthood?
  • How does humility protect a man from pride in rank or title?

Discussion questions

  • What does “monk before warrior” mean in practical Christian life?
  • Why should a Knight Aspirant be tested in devotion before recognition?
