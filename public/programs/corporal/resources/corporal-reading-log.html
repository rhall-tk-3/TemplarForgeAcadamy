Corporal Reading Log

This reading log is designed for adult members in the Corporal program. It provides space to track reading completion, class preparation, attendance, fellowship, discussion review, and personal notes for each week of the course.

Corporal expectation: A Corporal must remain current with classwork, attend regularly, show active fellowship, and demonstrate conduct becoming of one preparing for greater responsibility and honor within the Order.

Member Information

Week 1: Why the Order Was Founded / Mission and Historical Need

Study the historical need that gave rise to the Templars and understand that their earliest identity was tied to protective service rather than privilege, legend, or personal glory.

Key terms: First Crusade, Pilgrimage, Jerusalem, Protection, Mission
Memory targets:
  • The First Crusade took place from 1095 to 1099.
  • Pilgrims traveling to Jerusalem faced serious danger.
  • The earliest Templar mission centered on protection and religious purpose.

Week 2: Founders, Early Support, and Legitimacy

Understand who mattered in the early years, why formal support was necessary, and how legitimacy, rule, and discipline strengthened the mission of the order.

Key terms: Hugh de Payns, Baldwin II, Temple Mount, Council of Troyes, Bernard of Clairvaux
Memory targets:
  • Hugh de Payns is the founder most closely linked to the order.
  • Baldwin II granted the Templars quarters near the Temple Mount.
  • Formal recognition at Troyes and later privileges increased legitimacy and growth.

Week 3: Growth, Expansion, and Public Trust

Learn how trust, discipline, and stewardship supported expansion, and why spiritual purpose had to remain stronger than reputation, wealth, or power.

Key terms: Public Trust, Infrastructure, Stewardship, Integrity, Mission
Memory targets:
  • The Templars gained support through service, discipline, and reliability.
  • Roads, strongholds, and logistics helped support pilgrim protection and wider operations.
  • Growth required integrity, not merely efficiency.

Week 4: Structure, Offices, and Daily Life

Study the roles of knight, sergeant, and chaplain, along with offices such as Grand Master, Seneschal, Marshal, and Preceptor, and see how prayer and discipline shaped Templar identity.

Key terms: Grand Master, Seneschal, Marshal, Preceptor, Discipline
Memory targets:
  • The order included knights, sergeants, and chaplains.
  • The Grand Master, Seneschal, Marshal, and Preceptor held important leadership roles.
  • Templar life combined prayer, obedience, discipline, and service.

Week 5: Spiritual Discipline, Service, and the Corporal Rank

Focus on truth, courage, humility, discipline, charity, justice, accountability, and endurance, while also understanding that a Corporal must remain current in classwork, attend faithfully, and show active fellowship.

Key terms: Virtue, Fellowship, Accountability, Service, Corporal Rank
Memory targets:
  • A Corporal must remain current with classwork and attend meetings, Bible studies, and Round Tables.
  • Ranks exist for service and responsibility, not self-exaltation.
  • Christian knighthood requires integrity, humility, and faithful participation.

Week 6: Wealth, Power, Risks, and Mission Drift

Learn how growth, wealth, and influence created both strength and risk, and why stewardship, humility, and faithfulness to purpose were essential to the order’s long-term integrity.

Key terms: Commandery, Stewardship, Banking, Acre, Mission Drift
Memory targets:
  • The Templars became important as logisticians, administrators, and financial custodians.
  • Wealth and reputation increased both influence and danger.
  • The fall of Acre in 1291 weakened the order’s original military purpose.

Week 7: Crisis, Friday the 13th, and Suppression

Study the documented events of the arrests and suppression while emphasizing truth, justice, discernment, and the danger of false accusation under political pressure.

Key terms: Philip IV, Clement V, 1307 Arrests, Suppression, Justice
Memory targets:
  • The arrests in France took place on Friday, October 13, 1307.
  • Pope Clement V suppressed the order in 1312 under strong pressure.
  • The charges and confessions must be taught with attention to coercion and political context.

Week 8: Jacques de Molay, Fall of the Order, Historical Importance

Review the Templars from foundation to fall, understand why Jacques de Molay matters in the story, and teach Friday the 13th carefully as a real historical date connected to arrests but not proven origin of the later superstition.

Key terms: Jacques de Molay, Friday the 13th, Myth vs History, Legacy, Readiness
Memory targets:
  • Jacques de Molay is remembered as the last grand master of the order.
  • Friday, October 13, 1307 is a real historical date in the Templar story.
  • The later superstition of Friday the 13th should be taught cautiously, not as certain origin fact.

Final Summary
