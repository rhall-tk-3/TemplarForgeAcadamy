Program Purpose

The Corporal School provides adult members with a historical formation course that picks up where Levie ended and carries the Templar story through the order’s fall from grace. It teaches why the Templars were founded, how they gained influence, who and what mattered most in their history, and why they fell. The program also connects historical study to Christian virtues and the responsibilities of the Corporal rank.

Sources: Corporal School — 8 Week Lesson Plan Program, Britannica: Templars, Britannica: Clement V, HISTORY: Friday the 13th, National Geographic: Friday the 13th and Templar Myth.

Week-by-Week Path

Each week builds historical understanding while reinforcing the virtues, conduct, and discipline expected of a Corporal.

Week 1

Why the Order Was Founded / Mission and Historical Need

Week 1 explains the historical setting after the First Crusade and shows why the Templars were founded to protect vulnerable pilgrims. It introduces the Christian purpose of the order and connects early Templar mission to service, courage, humility, and protection of the weak.

Open Week 1
Week 2

Founders, Early Support, and Legitimacy

Week 2 focuses on the key people and places that shaped the early order. It traces the role of Hugh de Payns, Baldwin II, the Temple Mount, the Council of Troyes, and Bernard of Clairvaux in turning a small protective fellowship into a formally recognized religious order.

Open Week 2
Week 3

Growth, Expansion, and Public Trust

Week 3 examines how the Templars grew from a small religious body into a respected international institution. It highlights infrastructure, disciplined stewardship, and the need to preserve public trust while influence increases.

Open Week 3
Week 4

Structure, Offices, and Daily Life

Week 4 explains how the Templars were organized and why their internal structure mattered historically. It highlights the classes of the order, the major offices, and the daily disciplines that made the Templars more than a fighting force.

Open Week 4
Week 5

Spiritual Discipline, Service, and the Corporal Rank

Week 5 turns directly toward the virtues and responsibilities expected of a Corporal while still grounding them in Templar history. It connects Christian knighthood to prayer, charity, fellowship, accountability, and active service within the order.

Open Week 5
Week 6

Wealth, Power, Risks, and Mission Drift

Week 6 explores how the Templars’ success made them influential, wealthy, and vulnerable. It explains administration, finance, commanderies, and the dangers of mission drift when strength and reputation outgrow original purpose.

Open Week 6
Week 7

Crisis, Friday the 13th, and Suppression

Week 7 addresses the crisis years of the order: the role of Philip IV, the accusations against the Templars, the arrests of Friday, October 13, 1307, and the political and moral problems surrounding the suppression of the order.

Open Week 7
Week 8

Jacques de Molay, Fall of the Order, Historical Importance

Week 8 completes the Corporal historical arc by examining Jacques de Molay, the final destruction of the order’s public life, the historical meaning of Friday the 13th, and the difference between documented history and later myth.

Open Week 8

Historical Boundary

This Corporal section ends the main narrative at the fall and suppression of the Templars, including the arrests of Friday, October 13, 1307, the suppression of 1312, and the importance of Jacques de Molay and the final collapse of the order’s public standing. It does not move into the deeper source-analysis role reserved for Sergeant.
