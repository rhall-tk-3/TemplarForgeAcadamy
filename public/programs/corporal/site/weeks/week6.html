Week 6: Wealth, Power, Risks, and Mission Drift

Critically examines how the Order's success generated the very vulnerabilities that compromised its mission — international banking operations, vast land holdings, political entanglement, and the gradual erosion of the founding spiritual focus.

Lesson Focus Areas

  • The Templars' international banking operations
  • Vast land holdings and political entanglements
  • The loss of Jerusalem (1187) and the Acre crisis
  • Warning signs of institutional and personal pride

Guided Historical Focus

The Templars became extraordinarily wealthy and powerful — and that success became their greatest danger. Massive land holdings across Europe, complex financial services, and growing political influence pulled the Order further from its founding mission. The loss of Jerusalem in 1187 and the fall of Acre in 1291 exposed how far institutional pride and mission drift can carry an organization away from its purpose.

Memory Targets

  • Templar financial operations included letters of credit, loans to kings, and vast land revenues.
  • The Battle of Hattin (1187) led to the loss of Jerusalem under Saladin.
  • The fall of Acre (1291) ended the Crusader presence in the Holy Land.
  • Wealth and political power distorted the Order's mission long before its suppression.

Readiness Goal

Critically assess how success and wealth became the vulnerabilities that compromised the medieval Order. Identify at least three specific ways mission drift manifested historically, and explain the personal and institutional vigilance required to prevent the same erosion within the modern Academy.
