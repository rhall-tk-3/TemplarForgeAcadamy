Week 2: Founders, Early Support, and Legitimacy

Traces the key people and institutions that shaped the early Order — Hugh de Payns, Baldwin II, the Temple Mount, the Council of Troyes (1129), and Bernard of Clairvaux — and how formal recognition transformed a small fellowship into a legitimate religious order.

Lesson Focus Areas

  • Hugh de Payns and the first companions (c. 1119–1120)
  • King Baldwin II and the Temple Mount headquarters
  • The Council of Troyes (1128/1129) and formal rule
  • Bernard of Clairvaux and the Omne Datum Optimum (1139)

Guided Historical Focus

Understand who mattered in the early years and why formal support was essential. Legitimacy — through rule, papal privilege, and royal patronage — was not a luxury. It was the foundation that allowed the Order to sustain its mission. Obedience, structure, and shared trust were marks of Christian knighthood.

Memory Targets

  • Hugh de Payns founded the Order around 1119–1120 with eight or nine companions.
  • Baldwin II granted the Templars quarters on the Temple Mount in Jerusalem.
  • The Council of Troyes (1129) gave the Order its formal Latin Rule.
  • Bernard of Clairvaux's advocacy led to the papal bull Omne Datum Optimum (1139).

Readiness Goal

Trace the steps from a small band of knights to a formally recognized religious military order. Identify each founding figure by name and explain the specific role they played in securing the Order's legitimacy, authority, and rule.
