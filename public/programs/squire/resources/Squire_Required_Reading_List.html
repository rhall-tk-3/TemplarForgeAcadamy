Squire Required Reading List

Official website guide for the Squire School reading program, organized by Weeks 1–8 and aligned to the approved curriculum, reading-log expectations, and promotion readiness standards.

Program Purpose

The Squire School curriculum of the Knights of The Kingdom of Christ Order is designed to provide age-appropriate, formal instruction in the historical origins, organizational structure, and moral virtues of the Templar order. The program focuses on truth over legend, distinguishing historical facts from myths, and building a foundation of character through service. The readings support the five pillars of assessment: knowledge, judgment, service, character, and communication, preparing youth for eventual advancement to Sergeant.

How Squire Reading Works

The required reading for Squires is drawn exclusively from internal, school-approved materials rather than external bibliographies. This ensures youth-safe oversight and moderated learning. Students are expected to complete weekly reading assignments, participate in guided discussions, and document their progress. The readings align directly with the weekly lesson plans, the oral review rubrics, and the final promotion board expectations.

Official Required Reading Sources

The core curriculum is built upon a four-part reading stack. These official documents must be downloaded and utilized throughout the eight-week course:

  • Reading Stack 1: Order Structure
  • Reading Stack 2: Historical Timeline
  • Reading Stack 3: Simple Biographies
  • Reading Stack 4: Short Excerpts and Guided Readings

Weekly Organization (Weeks 1 – 8)

Week 1: Origins and Virtues

Required Reading: Short Excerpts and Guided Readings (Week 1 section) and Historical Timeline (First Crusade background).

Guided Reading Focus: Understanding the First Crusade in simple terms, why pilgrims traveled to Jerusalem, the founding purpose of the order to protect those pilgrims, and the introduction of core virtues.

Key Figures: None for this week.

Memory Targets: The First Crusade (1095-1099), the importance of protecting pilgrims, and the virtues of honesty, courage, obedience, charity, and reverence.

Readiness Goals: Demonstrate knowledge of the order's founding purpose and reflect on character through the understanding of virtues.

Week 2: Founding People and Places

Required Reading: Simple Biographies (Hugh de Payns and Baldwin II), Short Excerpts and Guided Readings (fact versus legend), and Historical Timeline (founding setting).

Guided Reading Focus: Distinguishing historical truth from invented myth (fact versus legend), and learning the foundational leaders and geographical locations central to the early order.

Key Figures: Hugh de Payns, King Baldwin II.

Memory Targets: The Temple Mount in Jerusalem as the early headquarters, recognizing fact over legend.

Readiness Goals: Develop judgment to separate history from myth, and knowledge of foundational figures.

Week 3: Approval and Mission

Required Reading: Historical Timeline (1095-1139), Simple Biographies (Bernard of Clairvaux and Innocent II), and Order Structure (religious order identity).

Guided Reading Focus: The Council of Troyes, formal support for the order, and understanding the identity of a religious order versus an ordinary army.

Key Figures: Bernard of Clairvaux, Pope Innocent II.

Memory Targets: Council of Troyes (1128/1129), Papal privileges (1139), timeline progression from 1095 to 1139.

Readiness Goals: Display clear communication regarding the religious nature of the order and firm knowledge of early dates.

Week 4: Structure and Daily Life

Required Reading: Order Structure and Short Excerpts and Guided Readings (service and discipline sections).

Guided Reading Focus: The internal classes, the administrative offices, clothing, symbols, discipline, and the principle of service before self.

Key Figures: None for this week.

Memory Targets: Classes (Knight, Sergeant, Chaplain); Offices (Grand Master, Seneschal, Marshal, Preceptor); discipline over display.

Readiness Goals: Understand internal structure (knowledge) and embrace the principle of service before self (service).

Week 5: Life of the Order

Required Reading: Short Excerpts and Guided Readings (Week 5 section) and Order Structure (service before self review).

Guided Reading Focus: Castles and roads for pilgrim protection, prayer and routine life, acts of charity and almsgiving, and forming a personal rule of conduct.

Key Figures: None for this week.

Memory Targets: The daily routine, the importance of charity, the concept of a personal rule of conduct.

Readiness Goals: Exhibit character by drafting a rule of conduct and readiness for dependable charitable contribution (service).

Week 6: Power and Crisis

Required Reading: Historical Timeline (Acre and later crisis) and Short Excerpts and Guided Readings (mission drift section).

Guided Reading Focus: Growth in power, the dangers of wealth and influence, the fall of Acre, and understanding mission drift.

Key Figures: None for this week.

Memory Targets: The fall of Acre (1291), the definition and danger of mission drift.

Readiness Goals: Exercise mature judgment when analyzing institutional failure and shifting purpose.

Week 7: Suppression and Courage

Required Reading: Historical Timeline (1307-1314), Simple Biographies (Philip IV, Clement V, and Jacques de Molay), and Short Excerpts and Guided Readings (justice and courage section).

Guided Reading Focus: The arrests, suppression, and end of the order, framed through the moral lenses of justice, false accusation, and courage.

Key Figures: Philip IV, Pope Clement V, Jacques de Molay.

Memory Targets: Arrests in France (1307), formal suppression (1312), execution of Jacques de Molay (1314).

Readiness Goals: Show respect and composure (character) when discussing tragedy and false accusations; solidify final knowledge of the timeline.

Week 8: Review and Board Preparation

Required Reading: Review all four official reading-stack documents: Order Structure, Historical Timeline, Simple Biographies, and Short Excerpts and Guided Readings.

Guided Reading Focus: Complete timeline review, map work, promotion board expectations, and service project presentation preparation.

Key Figures: Comprehensive review of all figures.

Memory Targets: Full timeline mastery, geographical locations, and core principles.

Readiness Goals: Complete communication readiness for the oral review and written quiz, demonstrating all five assessment pillars.

Reading Log Requirement

Please note that completion of a formal reading log is a mandatory requirement for advancement readiness. Squires must track their weekly progress through the four official reading documents. This log will be verified by the Schoolmaster prior to the final promotion board to ensure diligence, discipline, and honest completion of the academic material.

Parent and Schoolmaster Guidance

The Squire curriculum is intentionally designed to provide age-appropriate, supervised reading. Parents and Schoolmasters are encouraged to guide weekly assignments, facilitate moderated discussion, and confirm that the student understands the difference between truthful history and unsupported legend. Instructors should keep the tone instructional, youth-safe, and service-centered, avoiding romanticized treatment of military themes and emphasizing duty, reverence, honesty, and disciplined preparation for both the written assessment and the gentle, youth-safe oral review board.

Advancement Alignment

Diligent completion of these readings directly aligns with the promotion standards for moving from Squire to Sergeant (at age eighteen). A candidate's final evaluation will verify the following:

  • Successful completion of the eight-week Squire School.
  • Verification of the attendance threshold (minimum 85%).
  • Completion of memory and reading requirements (via the reading log).
  • A passing score on the written quiz.
  • Successful, composed completion of the oral review.
  • Verification of the service hours threshold.
  • Signature on the adult code of conduct (upon coming of age).
  • Final recommendation attached by the Schoolmaster.
