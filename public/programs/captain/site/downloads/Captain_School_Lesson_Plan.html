Officer Formation Level II

Captain School

Captain School forms the officer trusted with local command, transitioning from dependable junior officer to responsible steward of a body.

12 Lessons   |   Local Command   |   Commander / Preceptor Model   |   Final Board Review

Program Purpose

Captain School forms the officer who can be trusted with local command. It is the transition from dependable junior officer to responsible steward of a body: a man who can organize operations, preserve standards, mentor lieutenants, coordinate resources, care for the brothers under his charge, and sustain the life of the Order at the local level.

Historical Foundation

The historical model for Captain School is the Commander / Preceptor. In the Templar hierarchy, the preceptor or commander was responsible for administration and provisions, and commanders of local commanderies were charged with the day-to-day operations of houses, estates, castles, and jurisdictions under their care. These offices formed the practical backbone of the Order and provide the clearest model for local-command leadership.

Course-Wide Learning Objectives

Templar Identity

Explain how Templar commanderies and preceptories functioned and why local command was essential to the strength of the Order.

Officer Responsibility

Describe the role of a Captain as a steward of local operations, standards, people, and resources.

Leadership Under Authority

Demonstrate the ability to govern a local body in alignment with the Order’s mission and higher authority.

Character Formation

Practice humility, disciplined speech, consistency, prudence, and visible reliability in office.

Leader Development

Mentor lieutenants and emerging officers through example, correction, and delegated responsibility.

Promotion Readiness

Show evidence of readiness for wider oversight through practical local-command leadership, mentoring, and final board review.

12-Lesson Syllabus Overview

Lesson 1 — The Burden of Local Command

Focus: The Captain is no longer just a reliable officer under supervision, but a steward responsible for the health of a local body.

Leadership Emphasis: The Captain is accountable not only for what happens, but for what becomes normal under his influence.

Assignment: 1–2 page reflection on responsibility for a body rather than a task.

Lesson 2 — The Medieval Commandery

Focus: The commandery as a functioning local center of mission, discipline, logistics, and communal life.

Leadership Emphasis: A commandery was not just a place; it was an organized expression of mission.

Assignment: Short presentation or visual summary on the Templar commandery.

Lesson 3 — The Office of Commander / Preceptor

Focus: The preceptor/commander as the historical model for Captaincy, linking administration and provisions with local governance.

Leadership Emphasis: Command is not merely giving orders; it is sustaining the conditions for mission to succeed.

Assignment: Comparison paper between the Templar commander and your Captain rank.

Lesson 4 — Mission, Standards, and Local Identity

Focus: How a local body reflects the wider Order without becoming chaotic or rigid.

Leadership Emphasis: Every local body teaches the Order’s identity by what it tolerates and what it models.

Assignment: One-page local mission and standards statement.

Lesson 5 — Administration and Provisions

Focus: Calendars, communications, training rhythm, event support, logistics, and practical order.

Leadership Emphasis: Administrative order is not secondary to leadership; it makes leadership possible.

Assignment: One-month local operations plan.

Lesson 6 — Authority, Delegation, and Accountability

Focus: Governing through lieutenants instead of trying to do everything personally.

Leadership Emphasis: A Captain who cannot delegate will either burn out or stunt the growth of others.

Assignment: Delegation map for a local team or officer corps.

Lesson 7 — The Rule: Humility, Speech, and Order

Focus: Humility, restraint, disciplined speech, and the rejection of rumor, spite, and disorderly conduct.

Leadership Emphasis: The Captain’s speech becomes the climate of the command.

Assignment: 7-day command-presence log.

Lesson 8 — Care of Brothers and Pastoral Stewardship

Focus: Attentive care for the weak, newer, or struggling members of the brotherhood.

Leadership Emphasis: Leadership is not only structural; it is also pastoral.

Assignment: Two pastoral check-ins with written reflection.

Lesson 9 — Training Lieutenants and Building Future Officers

Focus: Multiplying strength by forming junior officers through mentoring, correction, and delegated responsibility.

Leadership Emphasis: The Captain’s legacy is visible in the officers he forms.

Assignment: 4-week mentoring outline for a Lieutenant or candidate.

Lesson 10 — Conflict, Discipline, and Restoration

Focus: Addressing disorder, weak performance, and interpersonal conflict with justice, restraint, and a commitment to restoration.

Leadership Emphasis: Correction should protect the brotherhood, not poison it.

Assignment: Case-study responses to local conflict scenarios.

Lesson 11 — Local Command Practicum

Focus: Leading a real initiative from planning to execution while organizing people, communicating clearly, and adapting as needed.

Leadership Emphasis: Command must be demonstrated, not merely described.

Assignment: Approved local-command project with after-action review.

Lesson 12 — Capstone Preparation and Captain’s Board Review

Focus: Integrating course learning for final portfolio and oral board evaluation.

Leadership Emphasis: Readiness for Captaincy must be visible in both conduct and command.

Assignment: Final portfolio submission.

Sample Oral Board Questions

  • Why were commanderies essential to the historical Templar Order?
  • What distinguishes a Captain from a Lieutenant in your Order?
  • Why is the Commander / Preceptor the right model for Captain School?
  • What must a Captain protect in a local body besides outward efficiency?
  • How do you know whether you are truly delegating rather than merely assigning tasks?
  • What does care of brothers look like at the local-command level?
  • How should a Captain respond when standards are slipping without becoming harsh or reactive?
  • How will you know whether your lieutenants are actually growing?
  • Where is your greatest weakness as a future Captain, and how are you addressing it?
  • Why are you ready—or not yet ready—for local command?

Practicum Requirement

The candidate must complete one approved local-command practicum, such as planning and leading a monthly command cycle, organizing a service project, coordinating a local officer team for an event or ceremony, establishing a training rhythm for lieutenants, or leading a membership-development effort. The practicum should include supervisor observation and an after-action review.

Promotion Recommendation Standard

A candidate should be recommended only if he completes the course requirements, demonstrates stable and disciplined conduct, shows competence in administration and local planning, leads the practicum successfully, passes the oral board, and proves he can sustain a healthy local body rather than merely perform as an individual officer.
