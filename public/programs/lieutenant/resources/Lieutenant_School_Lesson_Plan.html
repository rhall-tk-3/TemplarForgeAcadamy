Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ

Lieutenant School

Officer Formation Level I

Lieutenant School forms the first-level officer who can be trusted under authority; office is service, not status.

Core Overview

Course Purpose: Lieutenant School forms the officer who can be trusted under authority. It is the transition from committed member to dependable officer: a man who can receive mission, execute it faithfully, care for those assigned to him, maintain standards, and represent the Order with humility and steadiness.

Historical Foundation: The historical model is the Under Marshal, responsible for footmen and equipment, making him the clearest junior operational officer in the Templar hierarchy. The wider system emphasized chain of command, codified conduct, obedience, communal life, humility, discipline, and care for the weak.

12-Lesson Syllabus Overview

Lesson Title Assignment Leadership Outcome
1 The Call to Office 1-page reflection: 'Why do I seek office, and what must be purified in my motives?' Sees leadership as stewardship, not promotion.
2 Origins of the Knights Templar Short presentation or summary sheet on Templar origins and mission. Articulates identity and mission clearly.
3 The Templar Chain of Command Draw and annotate a chain-of-command chart for the historical Templars and your Order. Understands authority, reporting, and structure.
4 The Under Marshal and the Lieutenant Model 2-page comparison: 'Under Marshal and Lieutenant—historical parallels and modern application.' Understands the practical nature of lieutenant duty.
5 The Rule: Obedience and Mission Guided written response on obedience, mission, and self-will. Accepts leadership under mission rather than preference.
6 The Rule: Humility, Speech, and Bearing 7-day self-observation log on speech, correction, listening, and emotional control. Improves bearing, discretion, and self-command.
7 Care of Brothers and Fraternal Responsibility Interview a senior member and submit a reflection on what real brother-care looks like. Grows from task-focus into people-focus.
8 Readiness, Stewardship, and Reliability Build a readiness checklist for a meeting, event, ceremonial duty, or team operation. Learns preparation, follow-through, and trustworthiness.
9 Leading a Small Team Conduct a supervised team assignment and submit an after-action review. Can brief, assign, monitor, and complete a team task.
10 Correction, Conflict, and Discipline Case-study responses to leadership problems involving correction, lateness, or conflict. Handles minor problems with maturity and justice.
11 Lieutenant as Mentor and Example Mentor a junior member or candidate for one week and submit an observation report. Functions as a multiplier of standards and culture.
12 Capstone Preparation and Officer Review Submit final portfolio: reflections, assignments, readiness plan, and practicum summary. Demonstrates full readiness for first-level office.

Lesson Details

Lesson 1 — The Call to Office

Focus: Office as service, not status. Establish the difference between rank as honor and office as responsibility.

Leadership emphasis: Stewardship, motives, willingness to serve under discipline.

Assignment: One-page reflection on motives for office.

Lesson 2 — Origins of the Knights Templar

Focus: Founding story, mission, and why disciplined structure mattered from the beginning.

Leadership emphasis: Mission gives meaning to structure.

Assignment: Short presentation or mission summary.

Lesson 3 — The Templar Chain of Command

Focus: Hierarchy from Grand Master through regional and local command.

Leadership emphasis: Lawful authority preserves unity, accountability, and coordinated action.

Assignment: Chain-of-command diagram comparing history and your Order.

Lesson 4 — The Under Marshal and the Lieutenant Model

Focus: Under Marshal as the clearest Templar parallel for lieutenant-level office.

Leadership emphasis: Readiness, supervision, support of mission, dependable execution.

Assignment: Comparison paper on Under Marshal and Lieutenant.

Lesson 5 — The Rule: Obedience and Mission

Focus: Obedience as a core virtue; no officer leads well who resists lawful authority.

Leadership emphasis: Disciplined execution over personal preference.

Assignment: Written response to Rule passages on obedience.

Lesson 6 — The Rule: Humility, Speech, and Bearing

Focus: Humility, restraint, disciplined speech, and emotional maturity.

Leadership emphasis: An officer’s tongue can either strengthen order or damage it.

Assignment: 7-day self-observation log.

Lesson 7 — Care of Brothers and Fraternal Responsibility

Focus: Caring for the weak, sick, aging, and struggling members of the brotherhood.

Leadership emphasis: Authority includes moral responsibility for people, not just tasks.

Assignment: Senior-member interview and reflection.

Lesson 8 — Readiness, Stewardship, and Reliability

Focus: Maintaining preparedness, accountability, and stewardship of assigned duties or resources.

Leadership emphasis: Readiness is a moral quality as much as an operational one.

Assignment: Readiness checklist for a real activity.

Lesson 9 — Leading a Small Team

Focus: Briefing, assigning, supervising, and reviewing team performance.

Leadership emphasis: Clarity is charity in leadership.

Assignment: Supervised team assignment and after-action report.

Lesson 10 — Correction, Conflict, and Discipline

Focus: Handling problems directly, lawfully, and calmly without rumor or spite.

Leadership emphasis: Disciplined correction protects both people and order.

Assignment: Case-study response paper.

Lesson 11 — Lieutenant as Mentor and Example

Focus: Junior officers shape culture by example, even when not speaking.

Leadership emphasis: Your life is already part of someone else’s formation.

Assignment: One-week mentoring observation report.

Lesson 12 — Capstone Preparation and Officer Review

Focus: Integrating course learning for final practicum and oral board.

Leadership emphasis: Readiness for office must be visible, testable, and defendable.

Assignment: Final portfolio submission.

Sample Oral Board Questions

  • Why does a Templar-inspired officer need history?
  • Why is obedience foundational to leadership?
  • What distinguishes a Lieutenant from a senior member without office?
  • Why is the Under Marshal the best historical parallel for this rank?
  • What does it mean for a Lieutenant to care for the brothers?
  • Where is your greatest growth area as a future officer?
  • How would you respond to lateness, disorder, or poor communication in your team?

Practicum Requirement

The candidate must complete one approved practicum, such as leading a ceremonial team, organizing a service project, running a training segment, coordinating a meeting or officer support function, or managing readiness for an event or formal gathering. The practicum should include supervisor observation and an after-action review.

Promotion Recommendation Standard

A candidate should be recommended only if he completes the course requirements, demonstrates steady conduct, performs competently in practicum, passes the board review, and shows the character expected of a first-level officer.
