Major School

Officer Formation Level III

Welcome to Major School, the third level of officer formation within the Knights Templar of the Kingdom of Christ (KTKC). This advanced syllabus is designed to elevate seasoned local leaders into capable regional stewards, equipping them with the historical perspective, administrative acumen, and pastoral wisdom required to oversee multiple commanderies and foster long-term order growth.

Course Purpose

Major School forms the officer who can be trusted with wider oversight. It represents the critical transition from being a local commander—focused on a single body—to a regional steward responsible for multiple bodies, systemic alignment, and deliberate leader development.

Historical Foundation

This course is historically modeled on the Templar Provincial Master (or Grand Prior), who governed extensive European districts, managed regional revenue and recruitment, and oversaw numerous local commanderies to ensure they supported the broader mission of the Order.

Learning Objectives

  • Templar Identity: Understand and embody the role of provincial leadership.
  • Officer Responsibility: Act as a faithful steward over multiple bodies.
  • Leadership Under Authority: Maintain strict alignment with the Order's overarching mission.
  • Character Formation: Cultivate prudence, deep humility, and uncompromising justice.
  • Leader Development: Actively coach and mentor local Captains.
  • Promotion Readiness: Provide concrete evidence of regional assessment and strategic capability.

Detailed Lesson Plan

Lesson 1: The Burden of Wider Oversight

Formation Summary: Explores the shift in mindset required when moving from direct, local command to indirect, regional influence.

Emphasis: Recognizing that regional leadership requires patience, delegation, and a focus on systemic health rather than micromanagement.

Lesson 2: The Provincial Structure of the Templars

Formation Summary: A historical study of how the medieval Templars organized their European provinces to support the front lines in the East.

Emphasis: Understanding logistics, supply chains, and the necessity of regional support networks.

Lesson 3: The Office of Provincial Master / Grand Prior

Formation Summary: Detailed examination of the historical duties, authority, and limitations of the Provincial Master.

Emphasis: Balancing high authority with absolute obedience to the Grand Master and the Rule.

Lesson 4: Mission Alignment Across Multiple Bodies

Formation Summary: Strategies for ensuring that disparate local commanderies remain unified in purpose and doctrine.

Emphasis: Crafting clear directives and mitigating 'mission drift' at the local level.

Lesson 5: Inspection, Accountability, and Standards

Formation Summary: The theology and practice of conducting fair, thorough, and constructive regional inspections.

Emphasis: Using audits and accountability to build up local bodies, not merely to police them.

Lesson 6: Recruitment, Growth, and Officer Pipeline

Formation Summary: Shifting focus from immediate personnel needs to long-term talent identification and succession planning.

Emphasis: Building robust pipelines to ensure every local body has capable future Lieutenants and Captains.

Lesson 7: Resource Stewardship and Regional Planning

Formation Summary: Managing regional finances, properties, and resources with utmost integrity and strategic foresight.

Emphasis: Creating 6-to-12-month plans that allocate resources to areas of greatest need and potential.

Lesson 8: Training Captains and Strengthening Local Command

Formation Summary: The Major's primary pastoral role: serving as a mentor, shield, and guide for local Captains.

Emphasis: Conducting structured leadership reviews and providing actionable coaching to local commanders.

Lesson 9: Communication, Coordination, and Order

Formation Summary: Establishing clear, efficient, and charitable lines of communication between local bodies and high command.

Emphasis: Drafting regional communication protocols that reduce confusion and promote fraternal unity.

Lesson 10: Correction, Intervention, and Restoration at the Regional Level

Formation Summary: Navigating complex conflicts, moral failings, or systemic breakdowns within or between commanderies.

Emphasis: Applying justice with mercy, managing crisis intervention, and prioritizing spiritual restoration.

Lesson 11: Regional Oversight Practicum

Formation Summary: Active execution of a significant regional initiative under the observation of senior leadership.

Emphasis: Translating theory into practice through a real-world regional assessment or growth project.

Lesson 12: Capstone Preparation and Major’s Board Review

Formation Summary: Compilation of all coursework, final reflections, and preparation for the rigorous oral board examination.

Emphasis: Demonstrating comprehensive readiness to assume the mantle of regional stewardship.

Required Assignments Summary

  • 1–2 page reflection on multiple-body responsibility.
  • Presentation on Templar provincial structure.
  • Comparison paper: Provincial Master vs. Modern Major.
  • Regional alignment statement (one page).
  • Local-body assessment template.
  • 6-month regional growth and officer-development plan.
  • Regional support and priority map.
  • Structured leadership review with a Captain.
  • Regional communication protocol.
  • Case-study responses for three distinct regional oversight scenarios.
  • After-action review (AAR) of the regional assessment project.
  • Final portfolio (compiling all reflections, tools, and practicum summary).

Leadership Outcomes

Upon completion, the candidate will demonstrate the ability to think systemically, manage resources across a district, mentor subordinate commanders with wisdom, and maintain strict alignment with the Order's overarching strategic and spiritual goals. The candidate will transition from an operator to a developer of leaders.

Regional Oversight Practicum Requirement

Candidates must successfully complete one approved regional oversight task. Acceptable tasks include conducting a structured review of multiple local bodies, spearheading a regional officer-development effort, or executing a comprehensive district growth plan. This practicum must be conducted with direct supervisor observation and conclude with a formal after-action review (AAR).

Sample Oral Board Questions

  1. How does the burden of a Major differ fundamentally from the burden of a Captain regarding daily operations?
  2. In evaluating the health of a local commandery, what specific indicators suggest that the Captain requires immediate coaching?
  3. Describe the historical role of the Provincial Master. How do you intend to apply that historical model to modern regional resource prioritization?
  4. If two local bodies in your region develop conflicting cultures or interpretations of the Rule, how will you intervene to restore alignment?
  5. Explain your strategy for identifying and developing the next generation of Lieutenants across a wide geographic area.
  6. How do you maintain your own spiritual humility and obedience when operating with significant regional autonomy?

Promotion Recommendation Standard

A candidate shall be recommended for promotion to Major only if he completes all syllabus requirements, demonstrates disciplined and measured conduct, shows clear competence in regional oversight, strategic planning, and leader development, leads the practicum successfully, and passes the oral board. Above all, he must prove he can strengthen multiple bodies simultaneously, rather than merely excelling in one isolated local setting.
