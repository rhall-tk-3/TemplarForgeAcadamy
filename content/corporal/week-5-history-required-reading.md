# Week 5 - History Required Reading

## Required Reading

Read: [Templars - Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Templars)

## Primary Focus

The Templars at their height: wealth, banking, political power, and the seeds of resentment.

## Key Historical Subjects

The growth of Templar financial influence, the network of preceptories across Europe, money-lending and safe-conduct letters, and how power and prosperity attracted scrutiny.

## Formation Emphasis

Understanding how success without humility generates the very opposition that undoes an institution.

## Readiness Goal

Describe how the Templars' financial role expanded their influence — and explain the risk that accompanied that expansion.

## Historical Snapshot

By the mid-13th century, Britannica explains, the Templars had become one of the most powerful institutions in Christendom. Their ability to receive deposits, issue letters of credit, and move funds securely across great distances made them indispensable to kings, popes, and merchants alike. French and English monarchs borrowed heavily from the Order. This financial power was not separate from their military mission — it funded it. But it also created dependence, jealousy, and resentment. The very wealth that sustained the Templars' crusading mission made them a target.

## Key Themes

- Financial strength enabled the crusading mission but also invited political danger.
- Letters of credit and secure deposit networks were innovations born from military necessity.
- Wealth and influence attract scrutiny — especially when they outgrow accountability.

## Formation Insight

The Templars' expansion into banking was a practical response to the needs of the crusading enterprise. But institutions that become indispensable to the powerful rarely remain unthreatened. Competence without vigilance about perception is a vulnerability.

## Readiness Check

Before moving on, a corporal should be able to answer this in one clear sentence:

**How did the Templars' financial power contribute to both their strength and their eventual vulnerability?**
