# Week 4 - History Required Reading

## Required Reading

Read: [Templars - Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Templars)

## Supplemental Reading

For officer detail: [1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica - Templars](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Templars)

## Primary Focus

Knight, sergeant, and chaplain classes; key offices such as Grand Master, Seneschal, Marshal, and Preceptor; daily discipline and routine life.

## Key Historical Subjects

The Latin Rule, hierarchy, monastic schedules, and the separation of classes.

## Formation Emphasis

Embracing structured discipline and understanding one's organizational role without ego.

## Readiness Goal

Identify the major offices of the Order and describe the daily monastic routine of a Templar.

## Historical Snapshot

Britannica identifies three main classes within the mature Order: knights, sergeants, and chaplains. It also explains that the Order was headed by a Grand Master, that territories were divided into provinces under provincial commanders, and that each house, or preceptory, was headed by a preceptor. The brothers lived by a disciplined religious rule modeled after monastic life: they heard the divine office at the canonical hours, kept fasts and vigils, prayed regularly, lived in community, slept in a common dormitory, and ate together. They were forbidden to gamble, swear, or become drunk.

## Major Offices

- Grand Master — head of the Order, elected for life.
- Seneschal — second officer of the Order.
- Marshal — chief military authority, responsible for horses and arms.
- Preceptor — head of an individual house or preceptory.

## Daily Routine Themes

- Prayer ordered the day.
- Hierarchy ordered responsibility.
- Community life restrained ego and promoted obedience.
- Discipline shaped both spiritual and military readiness.

## Formation Insight

The Templar life was not heroic improvisation. It was ordered discipline. Every class had its place, every office had its duty, and every brother was expected to submit to a common rule.
